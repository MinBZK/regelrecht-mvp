## Context

A specific law says who may apply for a decision and what the application contains. The Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb) adds what every application contains and what follows once it is received. For the zorgtoeslag:

- Awir 15 lid 1: a tegemoetkoming is applied for at the Dienst Toeslagen, for a berekeningsjaar. Lid 3 asks the partner to sign too.
- Awb 4:2 lid 1: "De aanvraag wordt ondertekend en bevat ten minste: a. de naam en het adres van de aanvrager; b. de dagtekening; c. een aanduiding van de beschikking die wordt gevraagd."
- Awb 4:13 lid 1: the decision is due within the statutory or a reasonable period "na ontvangst van de aanvraag".

Awir 15 does not mention Awb 4:2. It does not need to: afdeling 4.1.1 applies to every application for a *beschikking* (Awb 1:3 lid 3, 4:1).

An application is a legal act of its own. It is not a decision. The Awb attaches consequences to it that exist without any decision: its content (4:1 to 4:4), completing it (4:5), the decision period from receipt (4:13 to 4:15), the penalty for deciding late (4:17), and appeal against not deciding (6:2).

### What the model did before

[RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) lets an article hook onto another article, filtered on what that article `produces`. [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) models the application as the first stage, `AANVRAAG`, of a *beschikking*, and puts the decision period in a hook on that stage. That causes three problems:

1. A hook on `AANVRAAG` only fires when the engine executes the decision from that stage. No caller does that. Hooks for Awb 4:2 and 4:13 there never run.
2. The application only carries what the decision article reads. The partner's signature (Awir 15 lid 3) falls out, because Zorgtoeslagwet 2 does not read it.
3. A request for a regulation of general scope has no `AANVRAAG` stage. That is right, but only by accident of the procedure table.

The chronolex proof of concept composed the application in the cell runtime instead. On 30 September 2026 the owner of this work rejected that: the shape must come from executing the law.

## Decision

An article can establish an application, and a hook can apply to one. The engine executes that article with its hooks. The shape of the application is what that execution declares.

### 1. An article says that it establishes an application

`produces` gets an optional block `submission`:

```yaml
# Awir 15
- number: '15'
  machine_readable:
    execution:
      produces:
        submission:
          kind: AANVRAAG            # lid 1: a tegemoetkoming is applied for
        legal_character: TOETS
        decision_type: GEEN_BESLUIT
      parameters:
        - name: aangevraagd_berekeningsjaar
          type: number
          origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: 'algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15 lid 1', rol: TIJDVAK}
        - ...
```

`kind` names what the belanghebbende submits. This RFC defines `AANVRAAG` (Awb 1:3 lid 3). Later the same block can carry `BEZWAARSCHRIFT` (Awb 6:4, 6:5) and `BEROEPSCHRIFT`. The Awb treats those the same way: required content, and a period from submission. One block keeps one filter and one index in the engine. The key is English, the value is the legal term, as with `BESCHIKKING`.

The block does not change the article's own outputs. It adds that the application comes into being here.

The decision article names the application it decides on:

```yaml
# Zorgtoeslagwet 2
- number: '2'
  machine_readable:
    execution:
      produces:
        legal_character: BESCHIKKING
        decision_type: TOEKENNING
        procedure_id: tegemoetkoming
        decides_on:
          - algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15
```

The engine keeps a reverse index from each such article to the legal characters of the decisions taken on it. So the Awir 15 application is an application for a *beschikking*, because a *beschikking* is taken on it.

### 2. A hook can apply to an application

Three filter fields join those of RFC-007:

| Filter field | Matches against |
|---|---|
| `submission` | `produces.submission.kind` of the executing article |
| `decided_by` | the legal character of every decision that `decides_on` the executing article |
| `established_by` | the executing article itself, `<regulation>#<article>` |

```yaml
# Awb 4:2
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
      decided_by: BESCHIKKING
```

Rules:

- A hook names `legal_character` or `submission`, not both. A hook on a submission takes no `stage` or `decision_type`.
- Filters are AND-combined, as in RFC-007.
- `decided_by` matches only if every decision taken on the submission matches. The engine does not guess which Awb regime applies.
- A submission with no modeled decision matches no `decided_by` hook.

`established_by` is for policy about one law's application. The fictitious Dienst Toeslagen policy asks for the account number, only on the Awir 15 application:

```yaml
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
      established_by: algemene_wet_inkomensafhankelijke_regelingen#15
```

The RFC-007 hooks index, keyed on `(hook_point, legal_character)`, gets a second key `(hook_point, submission)`.

### 3. The shape of an application is what its execution declares

Executing an article with a `submission` block fires its hooks. Every parameter of the article and the fired hooks belongs to the application, with its origin ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)).

`execute_stage` on such an article collects these parameters. If a required one is missing, it does not fail. It yields the missing names and the whole model: the articles in firing order, the decisions taken on it, and each parameter with the article that asks for it and whether it was supplied. Called with nothing, it yields the shape. Called complete, it computes, and the result also carries the model. A missing optional parameter is unknown ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)). The model and the execution use the same hook resolution, so they cannot disagree.

The hooks also fire when another article reads an output of the establishing article. That goes through ordinary evaluation, which cannot yield. So a hook parameter the caller does not pass must be `required: false`, or the call fails.

The cell in `packages/cel` executes Awir 15 with an empty application. It records as fields the parameters with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)). Its stream names only the event and the establishing article.

The decision requested adds its own fields. These are the parameters of its article with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL`, except the period. Awir 15 lid 2 asks the applicant for "de voor de beslissing op de aanvraag benodigde informatie". No article lists these data, so such a field has no grondslag of its own in `field_basis`. Only an application that requests that decision has the field. The rent belongs on an application for huurtoeslag, not on one for zorgtoeslag. With one decision on the application its fields are always there. With several, the shape returns every field and marks each with the decisions that ask it (`for_decisions`). The cell refuses a field that the requested decision does not ask. The decision reads the field from the application, like every other field.

### 4. What the Awb computes on an application

A `post_actions` hook on an application computes what follows from it, as Awb 6:8 does for the objection period. Awb 4:13 is such a hook. Here it only carries the moment that counts: receipt (`produces.moment`, see RFC-049). A specific law with its own period overrides the output through RFC-007's lex specialis.

### 5. Relation to RFC-008

RFC-008 keeps its stages, including `AANVRAAG` as the phase in which an application is pending. A hook about the application itself (content, receipt, decision period) now uses `submission`, not `stage: AANVRAAG`. RFC-008 gets a note pointing here. Its text stays as written.

RFC-008 also gained `is` on a stage, for the same case. With it, the Awir procedure `tegemoetkoming` says that VOORSCHOT and TOEKENNING are each a `BESLUIT`, so the Awb decision hooks fire on both.

### 6. Schema

Law schema v0.8.0 has `produces.submission` (`kind`), `produces.decides_on` and the three filter fields. Laws without a `submission` block are unaffected.

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as in the Awb. It does not need a decision to be modeled, executed or taken.
- The Awb applies itself through existing hooks. No specific law has to name it, and every specific law gets the same Awb core.
- An Awb change reaches every application. If Awb 4:2 lid 1 adds a telephone number, one new Awb version adds the parameter from its start date.
- Each link sits where the text puts it. Awir 15 creates the application. Zorgtoeslagwet 2 decides on it. The Awb says what it contains.

### Tradeoffs

- A schema change, and a new key in the hooks index. A corpus on an older schema lacks the fields until it upgrades.
- An article can now have two roles. Awir 15 establishes the application and has its own outputs. Readers must learn that `submission` and `legal_character` describe different things.
- The Awb applies to an application only once its decision is modeled. Without that, the engine cannot tell whether afdeling 4.1.1 applies.
- More hooks fire. Each run of Awir 15 now fires Awb 4:2 and 4:13.

### Alternatives Considered

**The application as the first stage of the decision** (RFC-008 as it stands). Rejected: the hooks never fire, and the application only carries what the decision reads.

**The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2` on Awir 15). Simple, but the text does not say it. Every specific law would repeat it, and one that forgets loses the Awb fields silently.

**Composition in the runtime** from blocks with `extends: {stage: AANVRAAG}`. It removed five copies of the Awb core, but resolved the same hooks a second time outside the engine. Rejected on 30 September 2026.

**The application names the decision it asks for** (`requests:`). The application article does not say that. The decision article does.

**`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** Smaller, but an application is not a decision, and the legal character is decision vocabulary (Awb 1:3). It would also leave no room for the article's own outputs.

## Implementation in this pull request

Built in `packages/law-model` and `packages/engine` (PR #1679, carried into PR #1689): `produces.submission`, `produces.decides_on`, the three filter fields, the second index key, and the submission model on `execute_stage`. Schema v0.8.0 declares them. In the corpus, Awir 15 establishes the application, Zorgtoeslagwet 2 decides on it, and Awb 4:2 and 4:13 hook onto it. Not built: a decision period computed by Awb 4:13 (§4), and the other kinds of §1.

## Open Questions

1. **Awb 2:8 and 2:10.** These cover every electronic message between body and person, not only applications. A broader filter may fit better.
2. **Lex specialis for a field.** A specific law that asks less than Awb 4:2 ("in afwijking van artikel 4:2") needs to override a parameter. RFC-007 only overrides outputs. A specific law that asks the same datum in its own words uses `specifies` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)).
3. **Which version shapes an application.** The Awb on the day of receipt, or of recording? They differ only for a paper application entered later, across an Awb change.
4. **What an application must carry to be decided on.** The law names some fields (Awb 4:2 lid 1, Awir 15 lid 1 and 3), but not all data a decision needs. Awir 15 lid 2 requires "de voor de beslissing op de aanvraag benodigde informatie" without a list. Awir 18 lid 1 requires data on request. Zorgtoeslagwet 2 lid 2 computes with the toetsingsinkomen. The voorschot (Awir 16 lid 1) needs an expected income. So the model assumes the application carries an estimated income and the BSN, with no grondslag: no article names them. Partly answered: the cell now takes the data the requested decision asks of the applicant as fields of the application (§3), on the strength of Awir 15 lid 2. Those fields still have no grondslag of their own. Still open: may Awir 15 lid 2 serve as that grondslag? And what about data the decision needs that its article does not ask of the applicant, such as the estimated income, which Awir 16 asks through a hook?
5. **The position paper.** The cell records the establishing article as the chronolexogram's only `legal_basis` (Awir 15), and each field's basis separately in `field_basis` (Awb 4:2 lid 1 for the name, Awir 15 lid 1 for the berekeningsjaar). That matches the paper's single source article. Open: does the paper need a place for field bases, or is `field_basis` a RegelRecht addition?
