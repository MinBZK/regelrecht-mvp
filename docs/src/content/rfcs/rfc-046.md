## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) brings the vocabulary of the [Chronolexografie position paper](https://chronolexografie.nl/position-paper/): a cell keeps a chronicle of dated recordings, the chronolexograms. An earlier proof of concept put a chronolexography block in each establishing article, and most of it repeated the law. This RFC decides where recording lives and where a recording gets its shape.

## Decision

[Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography) shows this at work.

1. **An optional layer.** Recording lives in its own crate, `packages/cel`. It depends on the law and the engine; neither depends on it. Another runtime may record the same law differently.
2. **A cell is a directory** with `cel.yaml` (who it is) and `reducties/` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)). Identity cannot come from the law, so `cel.yaml` is the only cell configuration. The engine matches it against the law's `competent_authority` ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). A cell without it does not open.
3. **The law gives the shape.** A submission holds the parameters of its `produces` item and of the hooks on it, minus the `moment` ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). A decision holds its inputs, findings included, and its outputs. An executogram holds the parameters of a fact an article `requires` ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). The cell refuses any other field and a wrong type.
4. **A chronolexogram does not repeat the law.** Its keys follow RFC-022 §1.3 (`recording_actor`, `grondslag`, `fields`); the rest comes from the law in force on `recorded_on` ([Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#what-a-chronicle-holds)). Numbers are stored without loss (`regelrecht_law_model::stored`).
5. **The cell guards the chronicle.** It is append-only, in date order. One submission gets one decision, and a decision is replaced at most once. That last guard is ours, not an Awb rule. [Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#recording-and-deciding) lists every refusal.
6. **A decision is replayed, not explained from storage.** The cell retakes it on its own day, with the law and the chronicle of that moment ([Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#explaining-a-decision)). This assumes every source can say what it knew on a given day.

```json
{
  "id": "019a1b2c-…",
  "kind": "AANVRAAG",
  "grondslag": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
  "recording_actor": "obs_de_klimop",
  "recorded_on": "2026-10-13",
  "moment": { "parameter": "datum_ontvangst", "on": "2026-10-13", "declared_by": "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13" },
  "fields": { "eerste_verlofdag": "2027-02-01", "adres_aanvrager": null },
  "outputs": { "redelijke_beslistermijn_einddatum": "2026-12-08" }
}
```

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework holds no recording vocabulary. A jurist reads it without knowing what a cell is.
- An Awb article that asks one more thing reaches every record through one Awb version.
- One `record_submission` and one `decide` serve every procedure. A second case needs rulework and tests, no code.

### Tradeoffs

- No provenance per field is stored; what a decision used is known only by replaying it.

Open points are listed in [Open Points in Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **A chronolexography block in the law** (RFC-022 §3.2 `extensions`). Recording vocabulary in a rulework, mostly repeating the law.
- **A field list in cell configuration, or a chronicle-stream in `chronicles/`** (RFC-022 §1.3). Both drift from the law.
- **The provisions per field** (`field_basis`, `output_basis`). Repeats the law in every entry.
- **The cell runs the objection procedure** (`record_objection`, `decide_on_objection`). That put Awb 7:11 in Rust; it is now rulework ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)).
- **A decision taken ex officio** (`Cell::decide_ex_officio`) for a school's own holidays. Dropped 19 October 2026: holidays are policy.
- **The place of establishment in the cell's identity.** A place changes over time, so it is an executogram (author's decision, Tim de Jager, 18 October 2026). That fits the paper's definition ("de vastlegging van daadwerkelijke levering of afhandeling") loosely, but the paper calls its three types a first enumeration.

## Implementation

`packages/cel`: `shape` derives fields from the law; `Cell::record_submission`, `decide`, `record_executogram`, `explain`, `findings`; `Chronicle` holds the `Gram`s; `identity::read` reads `cel.yaml`. Cells under `corpus/cells/`, tests in `packages/cel/tests/`.
