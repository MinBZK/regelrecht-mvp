## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) gives RegelRecht the vocabulary of the [Chronolexografie position paper](https://chronolexografie.nl/position-paper/): a cell keeps a chronicle (*kroniek*) of dated recordings, the chronolexograms. An earlier proof of concept put a chronolexography block in each establishing article, and most of that block repeated the law. Two questions follow. Where does recording live, and where does the shape of a recording come from?

## Decision

### 1. An optional layer

Chronolexography is an optional layer on RegelRecht.

- The law format, the engine and the corpus work without it and do not know it.
- It lives in its own crate, `packages/cel`. That crate uses the law and the engine; nothing uses the crate.
- Leaving `packages/cel` out changes nothing else.

Another runtime may record the same law in another way. What follows is how this cell does it.

### 2. A cell is a directory

A cell opens with its directory (`Cell::open`, `Cell::in_memory`). The directory holds `cel.yaml`, who the cell is, and `reducties/`, its reductions ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)).

```yaml
# corpus/cells/obs_de_klimop/cel.yaml
name: Het hoofd van OBS De Klimop
category: het hoofd
```

Identity cannot come from the law, so `cel.yaml` is the one piece of cell configuration. The engine matches it against the `competent_authority` the law names ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)): an `INSTANCE` by `name`, a `CATEGORY` by `category`. A cell without `cel.yaml` does not open.

### 3. The law gives the shape of every recording

The cell records three kinds of chronolexogram. It derives the fields of each from keys the law already has, and refuses a field no article asks and a value of the wrong type.

| Kind | Recorded by | Fields from the law |
|---|---|---|
| Submission (`AANVRAAG`, `BEZWAAR`) | `Cell::record_submission` | The parameters its `produces` item lists, plus those of the hooks that fire on it, minus the `moment` ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)) |
| Decision (`BESCHIKKING`) | `Cell::decide` | The decision's inputs, with the authority's findings among them; its `outputs` and `derived` data |
| Executogram (`EXECUTOGRAM`) | `Cell::record_executogram` | The parameters of a fact an article `requires` ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)) |

One application, received on 13 October 2026 by a fictitious school:

```json
{
  "id": "019a1b2c-…",
  "kind": "AANVRAAG",
  "grondslag": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
  "recording_actor": "obs_de_klimop",
  "recorded_on": "2026-10-13",
  "moment": {
    "parameter": "datum_ontvangst",
    "on": "2026-10-13",
    "declared_by": "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13"
  },
  "fields": { "eerste_verlofdag": "2027-02-01", "adres_aanvrager": null },
  "outputs": { "redelijke_beslistermijn_einddatum": "2026-12-08" }
}
```

- **A submission** runs the stage named after its kind, so the Awb hooks on it run and their outputs go in `outputs`. A field left out is `null` when it can be absent ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)); otherwise it goes under `missing`, and the submission is still recorded (Awb 4:5). The cell fills `moment` and refuses it from the submitter. A submission may refer to a decision (`refers_to`): a field of type `decision` then gets that decision's id, and each other field the decision holds under the same name gets its value.
- **A decision** refers to the submission that `requests` it. The authority's findings are among its fields; which fields hold a finding is not stored, and `Cell::findings` reads it from a replay (§6). The cell refuses a value from the caller that comes from elsewhere (the submission, policy or a reduction), and a finding no article asks or of the wrong type. Every output of type `decision` that is not null, such as the new decision of Awb 7:11, becomes a chronolexogram of its own; the decision and those land together, or none do.
- **An executogram** refers to nothing and holds no outputs. It is recorded whole or not at all, because no law speaks of an incomplete one. The cell refuses a fact the article does not require, an article with no version in force that day, and a fact that holds a parameter `id`, a name the row of a reduction already uses. A cell records a fact only when the fact names it in its `competent_authority`, where the fact names one.

That a place of establishment is an executogram is the owner's decision (18 October 2026). The paper defines an executogram as "de vastlegging van daadwerkelijke levering of afhandeling"; a place fits that loosely, and the paper calls its three types a first enumeration.

### 4. A chronolexogram does not repeat the law

Its keys follow an RFC-022 entry (§1.3): `recording_actor`, `grondslag`, `fields`. Which article asks a field, which article computed an output and which policy filled a term follow from the law in force on `recorded_on` and from `refers_to`. The chronolexogram stores none of them.

A number is stored without loss, in the encoding of the law model (`regelrecht_law_model::stored`, see [law-model](/components/law-model)), so a decision read back compares equal to what was computed.

### 5. The chronicle keeps order

The chronicle is append-only, in memory or in a JSON Lines file, and refuses a chronolexogram dated before the last one. The cell also refuses:

- a decision on something that is no submission requesting it, and a second decision on one submission;
- a decision that would replace a decision that was already replaced.

The last refusal is our own guard, not an Awb rule; see [Open Points](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### 6. A decision is replayed, not explained from storage

`Cell::explain` takes a recorded decision again with tracing: on its own day, under the law in force then, with its own fields, and with the chronicle as it stood before it. It fails when an outcome differs. `Cell::findings` reads from that replay which findings the decision used; a finding that a policy or the cell's answer set aside is not among them. The replay rests on one assumption: every source a decision reads can say what it knew on a given day.

The cell contains no Awb article. It records and decides an objection with the same two calls as an application.

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework contains no recording vocabulary. A jurist reads law and policy without knowing what a cell is.
- An Awb article that asks one more thing reaches every record through one new Awb version; no list in the cell has to follow.
- One `record_submission` and one `decide` serve every submission and every decision, so a second case (the parking card) needs rulework and tests, no code.

### Tradeoffs

- The chronicle stores no provenance per field. What a decision used is known only by replaying it.

Assumptions and limitations of the cell are on [Open Points](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **A chronolexography block in the law** (the earlier proof of concept, an RFC-022 §3.2 `extensions` block per establishing article). Recording vocabulary in a rulework, and most of the block repeats the law.
- **A field list in cell configuration, or a chronicle-stream in RFC-022's `chronicles/`** (§1.3). Both drift from the law.
- **Per field, the provisions it rests on** (`field_basis`, `output_basis`). It repeats the law in every chronolexogram.
- **The cell runs the objection procedure** (`record_objection`, `decide_on_objection`, the first version of RFC-050). It put the order of Awb 7:11 in Rust and one Awb name in the cell; the procedure is now rulework ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)).
- **A decision taken ex officio** (`Cell::decide_ex_officio`), to record a school's own holidays as a decretogram. Dropped (19 October 2026): the holidays are policy, as in a school guide.
- **The place of establishment as part of the cell's identity.** Identity and place are different facts, and a place changes over time; it is an executogram.

## Implementation

`packages/cel` (`regelrecht-cel`): `shape` derives the fields from the law through the engine; `Cell::record_submission`, `Cell::decide`, `Cell::record_executogram`, `Cell::explain` and `Cell::findings`; `Chronicle` keeps the chronolexograms (`Gram`); `identity::read` reads `cel.yaml`. The cells of the two cases are under `corpus/cells/`. Tests in `packages/cel/tests/`.
