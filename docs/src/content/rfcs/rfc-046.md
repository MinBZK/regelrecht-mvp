## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) describes chronolexography: each legal fact is a dated entry, a chronolexogram, in the chronicle (*kroniek*) of the organization that establishes it. That organization is a cell (*cel*). RFC-022 §3.2 puts the integration configuration for this on the regulation itself.

A proof of concept did that, with a block per establishing article that named the type of chronolexogram and its fields. A rulework then needs words a jurist does not use, and most of the block repeats what the law already says. Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a says that the head of a school grants leave "op aanvraag". [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) records that as `produces.submission: {kind: AANVRAAG}`, and the Awb hooks onto it. Art. 13a asks only what the application holds; what the head needs to decide comes with the decision. That is enough to know what an application for vakantieverlof contains.

## Decision

A rulework contains no chronolexography vocabulary. The law says what arises; the cell derives the record from it.

### 1. The test for a key

Can you explain the key to a jurist without the words chronolexogram, chronicle, cell or chronolex? Then it may stand in the rulework. Otherwise it belongs in the cell. `produces.submission: {kind: AANVRAAG}` passes: "this article establishes an application". `type: submission` with a list of fields does not.

### 2. What the cell derives for an application

| The law says | The cell derives |
|---|---|
| `produces.submission` on an article | That a gram arises, of that `kind`. The article is its `legal_basis` |
| a hook with `applies_to.submission` | That the hook's article takes part. The engine names these (`LawExecutionService::submission`) |
| a parameter of those articles | A field of the application. The cell refuses a name no article asks |
| `produces.moment` with a `parameter` | The moment that counts. The cell fills it with the day it records the application, and refuses it from the applicant |
| `produces.moment.legal_basis` | The provisions the moment rests on, next to the article that asks it |
| the same parameter in two of those articles | One field that rests on both. A different type is an error in the law |
| `nullable: true` on a field the application leaves out | The field is absent (`null`, [RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)). A field that cannot be absent stays unknown, and the cell refuses `null` for it |

The cell executes the establishing article with the values supplied, so the hooks run, and records the outputs with the article that computed each.

This derivation is the cell's own. Another runtime may record the same law differently.

One assumption in it is not in the law: the day of receipt (Awb 4:13 lid 1) is the day the cell records the application. The applicant does not supply it, and the cell has no intake that dates a letter on arrival. An application received on Friday and recorded on Monday therefore gets a deadline three days later than the law gives, and the chronicle's clock (§3) does not let the cell record it on Friday afterwards. A cell with an intake can let it supply the receipt date, no later than the day of recording.

### 3. The record

One application, received on 13 October 2026 by a fictional school:

```json
{
  "id": "019a1b2c-…",
  "kind": "AANVRAAG",
  "legal_basis": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
  "holder": "obs_de_klimop",
  "recorded_on": "2026-10-13",
  "fields": {
    "eerste_verlofdag": "2027-02-01",
    "naam_aanvrager": "J. Jansen",
    "datum_ontvangst": "2026-10-13"
  },
  "field_basis": {
    "eerste_verlofdag": ["leerplichtwet_1969#13a"],
    "naam_aanvrager": ["algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2"],
    "datum_ontvangst": ["algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13", "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13 lid 1"]
  },
  "outputs": {
    "verlof_aangevraagd": true,
    "redelijke_beslistermijn_einddatum": "2026-12-08"
  },
  "output_basis": {
    "verlof_aangevraagd": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
    "redelijke_beslistermijn_einddatum": "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13"
  }
}
```

The example leaves out most fields. `field_basis` lists, per field, the articles that ask it. The moment also rests on the provisions that say why it counts.

The chronicle is append-only and has one clock: a gram dated before the last one is refused. The same day is allowed.

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework reads as law, without recording vocabulary.
- A change in the law changes the record. A new hook on every application adds its fields to every application gram.
- The cell has almost no configuration: who it is and where its chronicle lives.

### Tradeoffs

- The derivation is code in the cell, out of sight of the article. This RFC and the [Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography) page list the rules.
- A gram names its kind (`AANVRAAG`) and its article, not a name of its own. Two articles that establish an application are told apart by `legal_basis`.

### Alternatives Considered

**A block per establishing article** (the proof of concept). It worked, but it puts our vocabulary in the law, and nearly all of it can be derived.

**A field list in the cell's configuration.** It drifts from the law: a new Awb version that asks one more thing would not reach the record until someone edits the list.

## Implementation

- `packages/cel` (`regelrecht-cel`): `shape::derive` reads the shape from the law through the engine; `Cell::record_submission` checks the values, executes the article and appends a gram; `Chronicle` keeps grams in memory or in a JSON Lines file.
- Not built: decisions on the application, reading the chronicle back, objections. Later RFCs add them.
