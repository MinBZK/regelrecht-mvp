## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) brings the vocabulary of the [Chronolexografie position paper](https://chronolexografie.nl/position-paper/): a cell keeps a chronicle of dated recordings, the chronolexograms. An earlier proof of concept put a chronolexography block in each establishing article, and most of it repeated the law. Where does recording live, and where does a recording get its shape?

## Decision

How this works in practice: [Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography).

1. **An optional layer.** Recording lives in its own crate, `packages/cel`. It uses the law and the engine; nothing uses it. Leaving it out changes nothing else. Another runtime may record the same law differently.
2. **A cell is a directory** with `cel.yaml` (who it is) and `reducties/` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)). Identity cannot come from the law, so `cel.yaml` is the only cell configuration. The engine matches it against the law's `competent_authority` ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). A cell without it does not open.
3. **The law gives the shape.** A submission holds the parameters of its `produces` item and of the hooks on it, minus the `moment` ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). A decision holds its inputs, findings included, and its outputs. An executogram holds the parameters of a fact an article `requires` ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). The cell refuses any other field and a wrong type.
4. **A chronolexogram does not repeat the law.** Its keys follow RFC-022 §1.3 (`recording_actor`, `grondslag`, `fields`). Bases, sources and which field holds a finding follow from the law in force on `recorded_on`. Numbers are stored without loss (`regelrecht_law_model::stored`).
5. **The cell guards the chronicle.** Append-only; nothing dated before the last entry. The cell refuses a decision on anything but a submission that requests it, a second decision on one submission, and replacing a decision twice (our guard, not an Awb rule).
6. **A decision is replayed, not explained from storage.** `Cell::explain` retakes it on its own day with the law and the chronicle of that moment, and fails when an outcome differs. `Cell::findings` reads from the replay which findings counted. This assumes every source can say what it knew on a given day.

```json
{
  "id": "019a1b2c-…",
  "kind": "AANVRAAG",
  "grondslag": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
  "recording_actor": "obs_de_klimop",
  "recorded_on": "2026-10-13",
  "moment": { "parameter": "datum_ontvangst", "on": "2026-10-13", "declared_by": "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13" },
  "fields": { "eerste_verlofdag": "2027-02-01", "adres_aanvrager": null },
  "outputs": { "redelijke_beslistermijn_einddatum": "2026-12-08" }
}
```

Refused on recording, besides the above:

- **submission**: a `moment` supplied by the submitter. A missing field that cannot be absent goes under `missing` instead (Awb 4:5);
- **decision**: a value from the caller that comes from elsewhere, and a finding no article asks for or of the wrong type;
- **executogram**: a fact the article does not require, an article not in force that day, a fact with a parameter `id`, and a fact whose `competent_authority` does not name the cell.

That a place of establishment is an executogram is the owner's decision (18 October 2026). It fits the paper's definition ("de vastlegging van daadwerkelijke levering of afhandeling") loosely, and the paper calls its three types a first enumeration.

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework holds no recording vocabulary. A jurist reads it without knowing what a cell is.
- An Awb article that asks one more thing reaches every record through one Awb version.
- One `record_submission` and one `decide` serve every procedure. A second case needs rulework and tests, no code.

### Tradeoffs

- No provenance per field is stored; what a decision used is known only by replaying it.

Open points: [Open Points](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **A chronolexography block in the law** (RFC-022 §3.2 `extensions`). Recording vocabulary in a rulework, mostly repeating the law.
- **A field list in cell configuration, or a chronicle-stream in `chronicles/`** (RFC-022 §1.3). Both drift from the law.
- **The provisions per field** (`field_basis`, `output_basis`). Repeats the law in every entry.
- **The cell runs the objection procedure** (`record_objection`, `decide_on_objection`). Put Awb 7:11 in Rust; it is now rulework ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)).
- **A decision taken ex officio** (`Cell::decide_ex_officio`) for a school's own holidays. Dropped 19 October 2026: holidays are policy.
- **The place of establishment in the cell's identity.** A place changes over time; it is an executogram.

## Implementation

`packages/cel`: `shape` derives fields from the law; `Cell::record_submission`, `decide`, `record_executogram`, `explain`, `findings`; `Chronicle` holds the `Gram`s; `identity::read` reads `cel.yaml`. Cells under `corpus/cells/`, tests in `packages/cel/tests/`.
