## Decision

Chronolexography is an optional layer on RegelRecht.

1. The law format, the engine and the corpus work without chronolexography and do not know it.
2. Chronolexography lives in its own crate, `packages/cel`. That crate uses the law and the engine; nothing uses the crate.
3. Leaving `packages/cel` out changes nothing else.

## Consequence for a rulework

A rulework contains no chronolexography vocabulary. The cell derives what it records from keys the law already has:

- `produces.submission` on an article: a chronolexogram of that `kind` arises. That article is its `grondslag`.
- The parameters of that article and of the hooks that apply to the submission (RFC-045): the fields of the application. The cell refuses a field no article asks.
- `produces.moment`: the moment that counts. It gets its own key, `moment`, and is no field. The cell fills it and refuses it from the applicant.
- Executing the establishing article runs the hooks. Their outputs go in `outputs`.

A field left out that can be absent is `null` ([RFC-036](/rfcs/rfc-036)). One that cannot be absent goes under `missing`, because an incomplete application is still an application (Awb 4:5).

Since [RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047), an article that is also the decision on its application runs only its stage AANVRAAG when the cell records the application: the hooks on the application, not the decision.

This derivation belongs to the cell. Another runtime may record the same law in another way.

## The chronolexogram does not repeat the law

Which articles ask a field and which article computed an output follow from the law version in force on `recorded_on`. The chronolexogram does not store them.

One application, received on 13 October 2026 by a fictional school:

```json
{
  "id": "019a1b2c-…",
  "kind": "AANVRAAG",
  "grondslag": "leerplichtwet_1969#13a",
  "recording_actor": "obs_de_klimop",
  "recorded_on": "2026-10-13",
  "moment": {
    "parameter": "datum_ontvangst",
    "on": "2026-10-13",
    "declared_by": "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13"
  },
  "fields": { "eerste_verlofdag": "2027-02-01", "adres_aanvrager": null },
  "outputs": { "redelijke_beslistermijn_einddatum": "2026-12-08" }
}
```

The keys follow an RFC-022 entry (§1.3): `recording_actor`, `grondslag`, `fields`. There is no `intake`, because the cell has none.

The cell assumes that the day of receipt (Awb 4:13 lid 1) is the day it records the application; the law does not say so.

The chronicle is append-only and refuses a chronolexogram dated before the last one.

## Alternatives Considered

- **A chronolex block in the law** (the earlier proof of concept, an RFC-022 §3.2 `extensions` block per establishing article). It puts recording vocabulary in a rulework, and most of the block repeats the law.
- **A field list in cell configuration, or a chronicle-stream in RFC-022's `chronicles/` store** (§1.3). Both drift from the law: an Awb article that asks one more thing does not reach the record until someone edits the list.
- **Per field, the provisions it rests on** (`field_basis` and `output_basis` in an earlier draft). It repeats the law in every chronolexogram.

## Implementation

- `packages/cel` (`regelrecht-cel`): `shape::derive` reads the shape from the law through the engine. `Cell::record_submission` checks the values, executes the article and appends a chronolexogram. `Chronicle` keeps them in memory or in a JSON Lines file.
- Not built: decisions on the application, reading the chronicle back, objections.
