## Context

Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 1: a claim to exemption for a holiday under art. 11 onder f "kan slechts worden gedaan indien het hoofd op aanvraag van de in artikel 2, eerste lid, bedoelde personen verlof heeft verleend". Lid 2: the head grants it "slechts eenmaal voor ten hoogste tien dagen per schooljaar", and never for "de eerste twee lesweken van het schooljaar".

One article, two legal facts. The parents apply, and the head decides. [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) made art. 13a establish the application, with Awb 4:2 and 4:13 hooked onto it. It left one problem open: a hook on a submission has no stage, so an article that is also a decision would fire the hooks on the application at every stage. The decision also needs values the application does not hold: when the school year starts, and whether the two facts of art. 11 onder f hold.

## Decision

Art. 13a stays one article, and it produces two things: the application and the decision on it. `produces` lists them (schema v0.8.1). Each item says what it is with `type` and names what belongs to it. The application says what it contains and which decision it asks for; the decision names its outputs. The decision follows the Awb procedure for a beschikking ([RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008)), whose first stage is AANVRAAG.

```yaml
produces:
  - type: AANVRAAG
    requests: leerplichtwet_1969#13a
    parameters: [bsn_jongere, eerste_verlofdag, laatste_verlofdag]
  - type: BESCHIKKING
    decision_type: TOEKENNING
    outputs: [verlofdagen, verlof_raakt_eerste_twee_lesweken, verlof_verleend]
```

### 1. `produces` as a list

- `type` is a kind of submission (`AANVRAAG`) or a legal character (`BESCHIKKING`, `TOETS` and the rest). An article lists one item per type at most.
- `parameters` names the article's parameters the item contains. For an application these are its fields, with those of the hooks on it.
- `outputs` names the article's outputs the item yields. A submission without it yields none, so at AANVRAAG nothing of the decision is computed. The decision without it yields every output no other item lists.
- `requests`, on a submission only, names the article whose decision it asks for: `<regulation>#<article>`, also when that is the same article (Awb 1:3 lid 3, 4:2 lid 1 onder c).
- `decision_type` and `procedure_id` belong to a decision, as in the object form.

The engine refuses a list with two items of one type, a parameter or output the article lacks, an output given to two items or to none, and `requests` on a decision. A `requests` to an article of the same law must name an article that takes a decision; one to another law is checked when it is used, since that law may load later.

An article that produces one thing keeps the object form, with `legal_character` and `submission`. Its meaning has not changed, and the corpus keeps it.

No item has a name of its own. An article and a type name one item, and an article reference is what the law itself uses to point at a decision.

### 2. Each produced thing has its own stage

When an article runs at one stage of its procedure, the stage named after the kind of submission (AANVRAAG) computes the outputs of the submission and fires the hooks on it. Every other stage computes the outputs of the decision, with the hooks on the decision at the stages they name: Awb 3:46 and 6:7 at BESLUIT, 6:8 at BEKENDMAKING.

| Stage | What runs |
|---|---|
| AANVRAAG | Awb 4:2 and 4:13; the application of art. 13a has no outputs |
| BESLUIT | the decision of art. 13a, Awb 3:46 and 6:7 |
| BEKENDMAKING | Awb 6:8 |

An article in the object form computes every output at every stage, as before. The rule for the hooks on a submission holds for it too: run at a stage of its procedure, an object with both `submission` and a legal character fires those hooks only at the stage named after the kind of submission. `LawExecutionService::submission` returns an error when that procedure has no such stage, because the hooks would then never fire. Run outside a procedure (`evaluate_law`, a BDD scenario, the editor), the article fires every hook, as before.

`LawExecutionService::execute_stage_at` runs one stage on a fresh state. Without a list of outputs it computes the outputs of that stage; with an empty list, only the hooks run. `LawExecutionService::execute_stage` walks the procedure the same way, so recording the application does not ask the head's judgement. `LawExecutionService::procedure_stage` names what the stage requires and the articles that take part. `Submission::stage` names the stage at which the hooks on the application fire.

### 3. What the decision needs, and where it comes from

The application holds the parameters its item lists. What the head needs on top of it are open terms of art. 13a:

```yaml
open_terms:
  - id: eerste_lesdag_schooljaar
    type: date
  - id: specifieke_aard_beroep_ouder
    type: boolean
    decided_per_case_by: het hoofd (artikel 13a, eerste lid)
  - id: verlof_valt_buiten_schoolvakanties
    type: boolean
    decided_per_case_by: het hoofd (artikel 13a, eerste lid)
```

The law says what is needed. Who supplies it is decided by the process. In order of preference:

1. **The application.** A field the application holds, by name. The cell finds the application through the submission that `requests` this decision.
2. **The recording actor's policy.** An open term a regulation fills with `implements`. The first lesdag comes from a fictitious policy of the school (`fictief_beleid_schooljaar_obs_de_klimop`).
3. **The deciding authority, at the moment of decision.** An open term with `decided_per_case_by`. The engine takes the judgement from the parameters under the term's id. A filling regulation wins, because it is the general rule, and the trace records that the judgement was set aside and by which regulation. A regulation that returns null for the case is silent, and then the judgement applies. The judgement wins over a default.

A date the stage requires (`besluit_datum`, `bekendmaking_datum`) is the day of the decision.

### 4. The decision in the chronicle

`Cell::decide` records one chronolexogram of kind `BESCHIKKING`, with `refers_to` set to the application's chronolexogram. That application is one under the article whose submission `requests` this decision. The outputs of art. 13a and of Awb 3:46, 6:7 and 6:8 land in the chronolexogram.

The chronolexogram does not repeat the law. Its `grondslag` is the deciding article. Which provision asks a field, which article computed an output and which policy article filled a term all follow from the law in force on `recorded_on`, so it has no basis per field or output. Where a value came from follows from the law and `refers_to`: from the application if the application holds it, from policy if a regulation in force on `recorded_on` fills it with `implements`, otherwise from the deciding authority or the cell.

The cell refuses a decision on a chronolexogram that is not an application requesting it, on an application already decided on, and a value from the caller that comes from elsewhere. The chronicle's clock keeps the decision on or after the application.

### 5. The test of art. 13a

- **Ten days.** Leave is leave from attending school, so a day counts when the child would otherwise attend: Monday to Friday. Holidays and school vacations inside the period are counted. This is our reading of "dagen".
- **The first two lesweken.** A lesweek runs Monday to Sunday; the first is the week of the first lesdag. Leave that overlaps them is refused.
- **Art. 11 onder f.** Two facts, both judged by the head: a parent has a profession of such a specific nature that the child cannot go on holiday with them within the school holidays (`specifieke_aard_beroep_ouder`), and the leave falls outside the school holidays (`verlof_valt_buiten_schoolvakanties`). Leave is refused unless both hold.
- **No school day.** Leave whose last day lies before its first, or that holds no day from Monday to Friday (a weekend only), is refused: there is no school attendance to grant leave from. This is our choice.
- Refusal is `verlof_verleend: false`. Awb 3:46 and 6:7 still apply.

Not modeled: "eenmaal per schooljaar" (it needs the chronicle read back), and lid 2's second sentence for a young person who is kwalificatieplichtig.

*Note:* [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) adds "eenmaal per schooljaar": a reduction of the cell reads the decisions back and asks the school's policy for the bounds of the school year.

## Why

### Benefits

- The article reads as the law does: one article, the application and the decision.
- The law says which outputs are the decision and which parameters the application. The engine needs no rule for an article that is both.
- The application names the decision it asks for, as Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder c requires of it.
- Each value of the decision traces to an article: the application, the policy or the authority's judgement.

### Tradeoffs

- A runtime that wants the application and the decision apart must run stages. A plain run fires everything at once.
- The cell runs the stages BESLUIT and BEKENDMAKING by name, and announces the decision on the day it takes it. It looks for a filling regulation without a scope, so a regulation scoped to a municipality is not found.
- On a term with `decided_per_case_by` that a regulation also fills, the cell accepts the head's judgement and does not require it; the engine's precedence decides which value counts. The chronolexogram records the value the engine used.
- The fictitious policy of OBS De Klimop holds for every school, as long as the law format has no scope for a school.
- The policy in force on the day of the decision gives the first lesdag. Leave in the next school year needs next year's policy.
- Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder c (`gevraagde_beschikking`) is still a field the applicant fills. `requests` could fill it; nothing in the law format marks that parameter as the one, and the cell does not guess.

### Alternatives Considered

**One object with both `submission` and `legal_character`** (the first draft of this RFC). The engine then skipped the article's outputs at AANVRAAG by a rule of its own. The list says it in the law.

**List items with `submission` and `legal_character` as in the object form** (an earlier draft of v0.8.1). The application did not say what it contains or which decision it asks for, and the two items had different shapes.

**An `id` per item, and `decides_on` from the decision to the application.** The ids are names the law does not have. The law points the other way: an application asks for a decision.

**A separate article for the decision** (PR #1689). Art. 13a has one machine-readable block, and the text does not split.

**Decision inputs as parameters of art. 13a.** They would become fields of the application, and the applicant does not supply them.

**A field on each value that says who supplies it** (`origin`). Rejected in RFC-045: process, not law.

## Deviations from earlier RFCs

The application must say what it contains and which decision it asks for (Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder c). Three RFCs of others are read differently here; their text is not changed.

- [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) (Accepted) matches a hook on `produces.legal_character`. A hook now also matches an item whose `type` is that legal character, and `applies_to.submission` an item whose `type` is that kind.
- [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) (Accepted) has the application as the first stage of the beschikking. Here the application is an item of its own that names the decision with `requests`; the stage AANVRAAG remains where its hooks fire.
- [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) (Draft) calls a decretogram an output with `produces.legal_character: BESCHIKKING`. It is also one of an item with `type: BESCHIKKING`.

## Implementation

- Engine: the stage rule for hooks on a submission and for the outputs of each produced thing, `execute_stage_at`, `procedure_stage`, `procedure_of`, `Submission::stage`, and `decided_per_case_by` read from the parameters. The law model reads both forms of `produces` (`Article::get_produced`, `get_decision`, `get_submission`, `outputs_of`, `parameters_of`). `LawExecutionService::requested_decision` and `applications_for` follow `requests`; the loader checks the list.
- Corpus: art. 13a with `produces` as a list; the fictitious school policy in `corpus/regulation/nl/uitvoeringsbeleid/`.
- `packages/cel`: the fields of the application from its item, `decision::derive`, `Cell::decide`, `refers_to` on a chronolexogram (`Gram` in code), with `grondslag` and `recording_actor` as RFC-022 names them.
- Schema v0.8.1: descriptions, and `produces` as a list of items with `type`, `requests`, `parameters` and `outputs`.
- Not built: "eenmaal per schooljaar", objections.

*Note:* [RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050) adds objections, with Awb 7:11 as the decision on them.
