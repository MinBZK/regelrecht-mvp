## Context

In the position paper a cell decides what it exposes and how it reduces its chronicle to a lexostatus. [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) §4.1 follows that. The compact cell in `packages/cel` declared its lexostatussen in a file `lexostatuses.yaml`, and a decision event named the one it read (`reads: aanvraag`).

That had two problems. The names and types of the values stood in configuration, so they did not trace to the law. And the law already declares them: Awir 15 and its hooks give the fields of the application, and the decision article gives its parameters. The configuration repeated the law, the same problem [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) solves for reduction.

The rule we follow: derive as much as possible from the law. Use policy where the law says nothing. Use cell configuration only as a last resort, and say why.

This RFC takes the thesis and the origin role `BESLUIT` from an unmerged RFC-047 on branch `poc/chronolex`. It leaves out that draft's `executes` and channel declarations.

## Decision

The law sets the interface of a cell: which values it must supply, with name, type and supplier. The cell decides how it gets them.

### 1. The interface is what the executing articles declare

For a decision, the cell supplies what the decision's stage asks: the parameters of the article and of the hooks at that stage, plus the stage's `requires`. The engine reports each one with its `origin` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)) and its article. For an execution, such as a payment order, the cell supplies what the executed article declares.

Example: stage VOORSCHOT of the procedure `tegemoetkoming` asks for the BSN, the berekeningsjaar, the moment of receipt, the expected income and the dagtekening of the voorschot, among others. No configuration lists them.

### 2. Some values the cell fills itself, because the law says which

| The law says | The cell fills it with |
|---|---|
| exactly one date among the stage's `requires`, or `produces.moment.parameter` | the day the decision is taken (`dated_by`) |
| `origin` with `rol: TIJDVAK` | the period the decision concerns ([RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050) §4) |
| `origin` with `rol: GEVRAAGD_BESLUIT` | the decision the application asks for |
| `origin` with `rol: BESLUIT` (§5) | the id of the decision the act is about |
| a date parameter of the application whose origin rests on the provision of `produces.moment` | the moment of receipt of the application |

Any other required value that nobody gives is refused. The cell does not guess.

### 3. How a cell arrives at the values is its own

RegelRecht offers three routes, in order of preference:

1. **From the application.** The cell reads the fields of the submission ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046) §3) from its latest chronolexogram in the case. A field missing from the chronolexogram is left out. It does not become `null`.
2. **From an article of the holder's policy** ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). Example: what was paid on a voorschot comes from `fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen`. The stream names the article an event reads (`reads`).
3. **From cell configuration**, as a last resort, with the reason stated.

A cell may also use none of these, for example a cell that queries a legacy system. It must still answer the same interface: same names, same types.

### 4. A lexostatus may be a record

Here a lexostatus is a record of named, typed values. Each value carries its provisions, its article, its provenance and its source chronolexograms. Whether a lexostatus is one value or a record is an implementation choice. The paper leaves it open, and RFC-022's own example (`openstaande_vorderingen`) is a list of records. The cell does not pick the fields. They are what the reading events ask, or everything it can give if no event reads it.

`Cell::lexostatuses` lists them: one per submission, one per policy article that reads the cell's register. `Cell::read_lexostatus` reads one by name. The [zorgtoeslag page](/components/zorgtoeslag-chronolexography#lexostatuses) lists them for that cell.

### 5. Origin role `BESLUIT`

A parameter with `rol: BESLUIT` receives the id of the decision an act is about, such as the toekenning a nabetaling executes. It is allowed only with `waarde: DOSSIER`. Two `BESLUIT` parameters in one article are an error, because the runtime cannot tell which decision is meant.

Schema v0.8.0 declares the role and the law model reads it. No article uses it yet. The fictitious payment policies use an explicit `refers_to` instead ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049) §5).

## Why

### Benefits

- Every value a cell answers traces to an article. A jurist who reads the decision article reads the interface.
- A change in the law changes the interface. If Awir 16 asks one more datum, every cell that supplies the voorschot is asked for it.
- The cell stays free in how it reduces.

### Tradeoffs

- This deliberately departs from the paper, which lets the cell decide what it exposes.
- Reading the latest chronolexogram assumes the latest one counts. A supplement (Awb 4:5) as its own chronolexogram would need its own rule.
- A cell cannot be asked for a value the law does not declare. Law or policy must declare it first.

### Alternatives Considered

**A lexostatus language in cell configuration** (RFC-022 §4.1, `lexostatuses.yaml`). Kept only as the last resort: it repeats names, types and legal bases outside the law.

**A process configuration per act** (the proof of concept's `process.yaml`). Rejected: it repeats law and policy, and nobody reviews it as law.

**The cell decides its interface, as in the paper.** Rejected: the law would then not say what a cell owes.

**The law prescribes the reduction** (`reads` in the article). Rejected in RFC-045: no law says how a register works.

## Implementation in this pull request

PR #1689 moves the zorgtoeslag cell to this design. The cell reads the application from the law (`Cell::lexostatuses`, `Cell::read_lexostatus`). `lexostatuses.yaml` is removed from the demo cell and the fixtures. What was paid on a voorschot is now a policy article. The §2 values are built. Role `BESLUIT` exists only in the schema and the law model.

## Relation to the position paper

The paper makes the cell autonomous over its facts and its reduction. This RFC keeps the reduction with the cell, but lets the law decide what the cell must answer. That is our own choice, for a system that executes law.

## Open Questions

1. **Several submissions in one case.** A supplement or change notice (Awir 17) may be its own chronolexogram. Does a decision read the latest chronolexogram, the latest value per field, or a policy article that combines them?
2. **Declaring the last resort.** Where does a cell record why it reduces in configuration, so a reviewer finds it?
3. **`executes`.** The proof of concept linked a policy article to the law article it works out. Should that come back, and under which name?
4. **Is a policy article a grondslag?** A grondslag must be literal in the text of a law. A beleidsregel (Awb 4:81) confers no power, and the organ may depart from it (Awb 4:84). Yet the fictitious policies cite their own articles as `legal_basis`, and the demo shows them as "Grondslag". Options: one field with a weaker name such as "Basis", or a grondslag (law) next to a source (policy).
5. **Derived grondslagen.** The zorgtoeslag application rests on Awir 15. It reaches the Zorgtoeslagwet only through Awir 1 and 2 and Zorgtoeslagwet 1 lid 1 onder e. Such a chain is not a grondslag. Should it be shown, for example as "derived through"? Does the law format need a key for it?
