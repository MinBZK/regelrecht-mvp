## Context

Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 1: a claim to exemption for a holiday under art. 11 onder f "kan slechts worden gedaan indien het hoofd op aanvraag van de in artikel 2, eerste lid, bedoelde personen verlof heeft verleend". Lid 2: the head grants it "slechts eenmaal voor ten hoogste tien dagen per schooljaar", and never for "de eerste twee lesweken van het schooljaar".

One article, two legal facts. The parents apply, and the head decides. [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) made art. 13a establish the application, with Awb 4:2 and 4:13 hooked onto it. It left one problem open: a hook on a submission has no stage, so an article that is also a decision would fire the hooks on the application at every stage. The decision also needs values the application does not hold: when the school year starts, and whether the two facts of art. 11 onder f hold.

## Decision

Art. 13a stays one article. It establishes the application and is the decision on it: `produces` carries both `submission: {kind: AANVRAAG}` and `legal_character: BESCHIKKING`. The decision follows the Awb procedure for a beschikking ([RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008)), whose first stage is AANVRAAG.

### 1. Each hook fires at its own stage

When an article runs at one stage of its procedure, the hooks on its submission fire only at the stage named after the kind of submission: AANVRAAG. The hooks on the decision fire at the stages they name: Awb 3:46 and 6:7 at BESLUIT, 6:8 at BEKENDMAKING.

| Stage | What runs |
|---|---|
| AANVRAAG | Awb 4:2 and 4:13, not the outputs of art. 13a |
| BESLUIT | art. 13a, Awb 3:46 and 6:7 |
| BEKENDMAKING | Awb 6:8 |

Run outside a procedure (`evaluate_law`, a BDD scenario, the editor), the article fires every hook, as before.

`LawExecutionService::execute_stage_at` runs one stage on a fresh state; with an empty list of outputs, only the hooks run. `LawExecutionService::execute_stage`, which walks the procedure from stage to stage, does the same at AANVRAAG: there it runs the hooks on the application and leaves the outputs of art. 13a to BESLUIT, so recording the application does not ask the head's judgement. `LawExecutionService::procedure_stage` names what the stage requires and the articles that take part. `Submission::stage` names the stage at which the hooks on the application fire.

### 2. What the decision needs, and where it comes from

The parameters of art. 13a remain the application. What the head needs on top of it are open terms of art. 13a:

```yaml
open_terms:
  - id: eerste_lesdag_schooljaar
    type: date
  - id: specifieke_aard_beroep_ouder
    type: boolean
    decided_per_case_by: het hoofd (artikel 13a, eerste lid)
  - id: verlof_valt_buiten_schoolvakanties
    type: boolean
    decided_per_case_by: het hoofd (artikel 13a, eerste lid)
```

The law says what is needed. Who supplies it is decided by the process. In order of preference:

1. **The application.** A parameter of art. 13a, or one the application holds by name.
2. **The recording actor's policy.** An open term a regulation fills with `implements`. The first lesdag comes from a fictitious policy of the school (`fictief_beleid_schooljaar_obs_de_klimop`).
3. **The deciding authority, at the moment of decision.** An open term with `decided_per_case_by`. The engine takes the judgement from the parameters under the term's id. A filling regulation wins, because it is the general rule, and the trace records that the judgement was set aside and by which regulation. A regulation that returns null for the case is silent, and then the judgement applies. The judgement wins over a default.

A date the stage requires (`besluit_datum`, `bekendmaking_datum`) is the day of the decision.

### 3. The decision in the chronicle

`Cell::decide` records one chronolexogram of kind `BESCHIKKING`, with `refers_to` set to the application's chronolexogram. Per field, `field_source` says where the value came from: `application`, `policy`, `caller` or `cell`. The outputs of art. 13a and of Awb 3:46, 6:7 and 6:8 land in the chronolexogram.

The chronolexogram does not repeat the law. Its `grondslag` is the deciding article. Which provision asks a field, which article computed an output and which policy article filled a term all follow from the law in force on `recorded_on`, so it has no basis per field or output. Where a value came from is no part of the law, so `field_source` stays.

The cell refuses a decision on a chronolexogram that is not an application under this article, on an application already decided on, and a value from the caller that comes from elsewhere. The chronicle's clock keeps the decision on or after the application.

### 4. The test of art. 13a

- **Ten days.** Leave is leave from attending school, so a day counts when the child would otherwise attend: Monday to Friday. Holidays and school vacations inside the period are counted. This is our reading of "dagen".
- **The first two lesweken.** A lesweek runs Monday to Sunday; the first is the week of the first lesdag. Leave that overlaps them is refused.
- **Art. 11 onder f.** Two facts, both judged by the head: a parent has a profession of such a specific nature that the child cannot go on holiday with them within the school holidays (`specifieke_aard_beroep_ouder`), and the leave falls outside the school holidays (`verlof_valt_buiten_schoolvakanties`). Leave is refused unless both hold.
- **No school day.** Leave whose last day lies before its first, or that holds no day from Monday to Friday (a weekend only), is refused: there is no school attendance to grant leave from. This is our choice.
- Refusal is `verlof_verleend: false`. Awb 3:46 and 6:7 still apply.

Not modeled: "eenmaal per schooljaar" (it needs the chronicle read back), and lid 2's second sentence for a young person who is kwalificatieplichtig.

## Why

### Benefits

- The article reads as the law does: one article, the application and the decision.
- No new keys in the law format. Schema v0.8.1 only changes descriptions.
- Each value of the decision traces to an article: the application, the policy or the authority's judgement.

### Tradeoffs

- A runtime that wants the application and the decision apart must run stages. A plain run fires everything at once.
- The cell runs the stages BESLUIT and BEKENDMAKING by name, and announces the decision on the day it takes it.
- The cell looks for a filling regulation without a scope. A regulation scoped to a municipality is not found that way.
- On a term with `decided_per_case_by` that a regulation also fills, the cell accepts the head's judgement and does not require it; the engine's precedence decides which value counts. The chronolexogram records the value the engine used. When that differs from the judgement, `field_source` says `policy`. When the regulation and the judgement agree, the cell cannot tell which one the engine took, and it says `caller`.
- The fictitious policy of OBS De Klimop holds for every school, as long as the law format has no scope for a school.
- The policy in force on the day of the decision gives the first lesdag. Leave in the next school year needs next year's policy.

### Alternatives Considered

**A separate article for the decision, with `decides_on` pointing at the application** (PR #1689). Art. 13a has one machine-readable block, and the text does not split. It also adds a key to the format.

**Decision inputs as parameters of art. 13a.** They would become fields of the application, and the applicant does not supply them.

**A field on each value that says who supplies it** (`origin`). Rejected in RFC-045: process, not law.

## Implementation

- Engine: the stage rule for hooks on a submission, `execute_stage_at`, `procedure_stage`, `procedure_of`, `Submission::stage`, and `decided_per_case_by` read from the parameters.
- Corpus: art. 13a as application and beschikking; the fictitious school policy in `corpus/regulation/nl/uitvoeringsbeleid/`.
- `packages/cel`: `decision::derive`, `Cell::decide`, `refers_to` and `field_source` on a chronolexogram (`Gram` in code), with `grondslag` and `recording_actor` as RFC-022 names them.
- Schema v0.8.1, descriptions only.
- Not built: "eenmaal per schooljaar", objections.
