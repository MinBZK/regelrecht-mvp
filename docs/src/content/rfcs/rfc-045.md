## Context

A specific law says that someone may apply for a decision. The Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb) says what every application contains and what follows once it is received. For vakantieverlof:

- Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 1: the head of the school grants leave "op aanvraag" of the parents.
- Awb 4:2 lid 1: "De aanvraag wordt ondertekend en bevat ten minste: a. de naam en het adres van de aanvrager; b. de dagtekening; c. een aanduiding van de beschikking die wordt gevraagd."
- Awb 4:13: the decision is due within the statutory period or, without one, within a reasonable period "na ontvangst van de aanvraag". Lid 2 puts the outer limit of that reasonable period at eight weeks.

Article 13a does not mention the Awb, and it does not need to.

[RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) lets an article hook onto another, filtered on the legal character of a decision. An application is not a decision, so there was nothing to hook onto. [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) has an `AANVRAAG` stage in the procedure of a *beschikking*, but a hook there only fires when a caller executes the decision from that stage, and none does.

## Decision

An article can say that it establishes an application, and a hook can apply to one. Executing the article fires those hooks.

### An article establishes an application

`produces` gets an optional block `submission`:

```yaml
# Leerplichtwet 1969, art. 13a
execution:
  produces:
    submission:
      kind: AANVRAAG
```

`kind` names what the belanghebbende submits. Schema v0.8.0 allows `AANVRAAG` (Awb 1:3 lid 3). A bezwaarschrift can later be a second kind. The block sits next to `legal_character` and does not change the article's own outputs.

### A hook applies to an application

`applies_to` gets two fields:

| Field | Matches |
|---|---|
| `submission` | `produces.submission.kind` of the executing article |
| `established_by` | the executing article itself, `<regulation>#<article>` without a paragraph |

```yaml
# Awb 4:2
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
```

- A hook names `legal_character` or `submission`, not both. The schema says so with `oneOf`, and the engine refuses such a law when it loads it.
- A hook on a submission takes no `stage` or `decision_type`. It fires whenever the establishing article runs.
- `established_by` narrows the hook to the application of one article, for policy that works out one law's application.

The RFC-007 hooks index gets a second kind of key, `(hook_point, kind)`, next to `(hook_point, legal_character)`.

### What the application contains

The application holds the parameters of the establishing article plus those of the hooks that fire on it. For vakantieverlof that is `bsn_jongere`, `eerste_verlofdag` and `laatste_verlofdag` from art. 13a, and the name, address, date and signature from Awb 4:2. What the administrative body needs to decide, such as the judgement under art. 11 onder f, is a parameter of the decision, not of the application.

### The moment that counts

`produces.moment` says which moment counts in law for what an article produces, with the provision that says so:

```yaml
# Awb 4:13
produces:
  moment:
    parameter: datum_ontvangst
    legal_basis:
      - algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13 lid 1
```

The engine does not read it. It is there for a runtime that records the application as a dated fact.

### The engine names the articles that take part

`LawExecutionService::submission(law_id, article, date)` returns the kind and the articles that take part: the establishing article, then each hook in the order the engine fires it. `RuleResolver::find_submission_hooks` answers the same question for one hook point. Both use the index and filters that execution uses. Unlike an execution, they do not report a hook whose declaration comes from another version of its law.

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as in the Awb. It does not need a decision to be modeled first.
- No specific law names the Awb, and every application gets the same Awb core.
- A change to Awb 4:2 reaches every application through one new Awb version.

### Tradeoffs

- More hooks fire: every run of art. 13a now fires Awb 4:2 and 4:13.
- Afdeling 4.1.1 of the Awb concerns applications for a *beschikking*. Without a filter on the decision taken on the application, Awb 4:2 also fires on an application for another kind of decision. Narrowing on the decision is left for later.
- A hook parameter that the caller does not pass must be `required: false`, or the execution of the specific law fails.
- A hook on a submission has no stage. An article with both a staged procedure and `produces.submission` fires Awb 4:2 and 4:13 at every `execute_stage` call. No article in the corpus has both yet. The slice that adds the decision on the application decides at which stage they fire.

### Alternatives Considered

**The application as the first stage of the decision** (RFC-008 as it stands). Rejected: the hooks never fire.

**The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2`). The text does not say it, and a law that forgets it loses the Awb fields without notice.

**`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** An application is not a decision, and the legal character is decision vocabulary (Awb 1:3).

**A field on each parameter that says who supplies it** (`origin`). Rejected: who supplies a value is process information, not law. The rulework says which data are needed; the structure above says which of them the application holds.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.0: `produces.submission`, `produces.moment`, and `applies_to.submission` and `established_by`.
- `packages/law-model` reads them and refuses an `established_by` with a paragraph.
- `packages/engine` files hooks on a submission in the hooks index, fires them when the establishing article runs, and refuses a hook that names both a decision and a submission.
- Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a establishes an `AANVRAAG`; Awb 4:2 and 4:13 hook onto it. A scenario next to the Leerplichtwet proves the result.
- Not built: a filter on the decision taken on the application, and other kinds of submission.
