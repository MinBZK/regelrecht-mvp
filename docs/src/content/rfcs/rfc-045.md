## Context

In [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) §4.1 a cell reduces its own chronicle to a lexostatus. §4.1 sketches a provisional place for that reduction in cell configuration, and leaves the format to a later RFC.

That format was built twice, in the chronolex proof of concept (`poc/chronolex`) and in the first version of `packages/cel`. Both times it became a rule language of its own, with `filter`, `exists`, `sum` and `pick: latest`. Such a language repeats `FOREACH` from [RFC-016](/rfcs/rfc-016). It has no type checker ([RFC-037](/rfcs/rfc-037)), no trace ([RFC-039](/rfcs/rfc-039)) and no conformance suite. And it sits in cell configuration, where a jurist does not look.

**Experiment A** (25 September 2026, branch `exp/reductie-als-engine`) ran every reduction of the proof of concept as an engine run. All 386 values in 180 cases matched. It was 50 to 100 times slower (0.21 ms against 2 µs per reduction). It also showed what the engine lacks. §5 to §13 take that up.

On 6 October 2026 the owner of this work decided where the reduction lives: with whoever holds the facts, as an article in the holder's policy, in the law format.

## Decision

The law says which register exists, who keeps it and who may ask. It never says how the register works. The query is an article in the keeper's implementing policy. The engine runs it. The consumer asks for it with an ordinary `source`. Only the binding of the register says which system answers. For a chronicle, that binding is in the cell configuration.

This amends RFC-022 §4.1 on the place and form of a reduction. The rest of §4.1 holds: the reduction stays with the holder, a consumer cannot inject its own, and synthesis happens at the consumer.

§1 to §4 place the query. §5 to §13 are engine changes it needs. §14 says where a query runs.

### 1. Four layers for a source

1. **A law sets up the register and names its keeper.** Kieswet G 1 lid 1: "een register dat door het centraal stembureau wordt bijgehouden".
2. **A law gives the consumer the ground to ask.** Wet op de politieke partijen 121a: the centraal stembureau gives the Autoriteit information from that register on request.
3. **The keeper's implementing policy describes the query**: parameters, outputs, and the search as engine actions.
4. **The consumer's policy fills in only what the law leaves open**, such as the key to query on.

The query article has one input without a source (`source: {}`): the register. The consumer reads its outputs with an RFC-007 `source`.

In the demo, the Dienst Toeslagen is both keeper and consumer. It reads its own chronicle with `fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen`. [Zorgtoeslag as Chronolexography](/components/zorgtoeslag-chronolexography#lexostatuses) lists its articles. Each has the input `grams` with `source: {}`, bound in the cell configuration:

```yaml
# corpus/demo/cells/toeslagen/cell.yaml
registers:
  fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen#kroniek: {chronicle: toeslagen}
```

The key is `<policy>#<register name>`. Each binding becomes one engine data source, scoped to that policy. A policy reads at most one register of a cell.

The source returns one row per chronolexogram that holds at that moment, in legal order (§7). A row has the chronolexogram's fields plus `id`, `event`, `type`, `subtype`, `stage`, `establishes`, `root`, `sequence`, `effective_at`, `effective_date`, `recorded_at`, `recorded_date` and `period`. A field the law gives the event but the chronolexogram lacks is `null`, so a policy can test for it.

The Kieswet only says "een register". The register's name belongs to the keeper. For now it is the second half of the binding key (Open Question 3).

### 2. Generic duties are carried out by the authority's own article

Awir 24 lid 2 sets off the voorschotten against the toegekende tegemoetkoming. Awb 4:95 lid 4 does the same for subsidy advances. A general law cannot say which administration holds a body's payments.

So the entry point is an article in the body's own policy or law. Its `legal_basis` is the general provision. It reads the payment administration through a `source`. The general law gets no `open_terms`, and the engine does not pick an implementation by `competent_authority`.

Example: an article of `fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen` counts what was paid on the voorschot for a berekeningsjaar. Only installments recorded as paid count, not installments granted.

### 3. Chronolexogram identity and references

There is no case (*zaak*) record. The cell gives each chronolexogram a UUID v7 id: rising in time and unique across cells. A chronolexogram refers by id to the chronolexogram it belongs to.

The text names the reference. A decision refers to its application as `on_application` ("besluit op de aanvraag", see `decides_on` in [RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)). An installment refers to its `voorschot`. The establishing article declares the reference, or the cell derives it ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049)). The cell refuses a chronolexogram whose required reference points at a missing chronolexogram or a chronolexogram of the wrong article or stage.

Following the references leads to a root: a chronolexogram without a reference. Every row carries its `root`, so a query can select on it. The group is named after its root ("application g-101"). This keeps RFC-008's rule that the besluit holds the state, without RFC-022's `zaakkenmerk`.

### 4. The text test

Every part of a binding (register, keeper, content, query key, name) gets one tag:

- **Text**: a law says it, with a quotation.
- **Policy**: a choice of the keeper or consumer, as machine-readable policy with an owner.
- **Assumption**: nobody carries it. Allowed in a proof of concept, visibly named.

We may write policy for an organization that has not adopted it. Its name, header and every article then say so. `fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen` has *aanname* in its name and marks each choice the law does not make. If the real keeper later publishes its own interface, the binding may point outside RegelRecht. Law and policy stay the same.

### 5. A nested FOREACH sees the outer binding

*Proposed, not built.* An inner `FOREACH` can read the `as` binding of every outer `FOREACH`. This replaces RFC-016's empty inner scope. A query needs it to join, such as each advance decision with its payments. A binding may not shadow another visible name. The type checker runs over the corpus before this lands.

### 6. Records

*Proposed, not built.* `{operation: RECORD, fields: {…}}` builds a record, so a `FOREACH` without `combine` returns a table. An `object` parameter, input or output may declare its `fields`, an `array` its `items`. The type checker then knows the type of `$g.bedrag`.

### 7. The latest element

*Partly built.* The cell returns chronolexograms in legal order: by `effective_at`, then `recorded_at`, then recording order. A late fact about an earlier moment is not the latest. Each row carries its position as `sequence`.

Today a policy finds the latest chronolexogram with `MAX` of `sequence`, then reads its value with an `ADD` over that one chronolexogram. The proposal is `combine: FIRST` and `combine: LAST`. The array input then declares its guaranteed order (`ordered_by: [effective_at, recorded_at]`). Without it, `FIRST` or `LAST` is a load error.

### 8. Local names in a FOREACH

*Proposed, not built.* An optional `let` binds names per element, so a subexpression is written once. It follows the shadowing rule of §5 and shows in the trace.

### 9. Absent, null and empty

*Proposed, not built.* A query must tell apart: no chronolexogram, a chronolexogram with an empty field, and a chronolexogram with a value. `FOREACH` gains an optional `empty`, used when no element passes the filter. Its `legal_basis` says why absence from the register means it did not happen. Today the row's `null` (§1) covers the empty field. No chronolexogram at all falls back to the identity of `combine`.

### 10. Sources per element

*Proposed, not built.* There is no `source` per element. A query needed per row takes a list of keys and returns a list of rows. A `source.parameters` value may be `null` when the target parameter is `nullable`.

### 11. The collection limit

*Proposed, not built.* `MAX_ARRAY_SIZE` becomes an engine setting. A runtime may raise it and records it in the receipt. The data source gets the query's parameters as criteria and returns only matching records.

### 12. Checks against the stream

*Partly built.* The cell refuses a binding to a chronicle no stream records in, and a policy that reads two registers of the cell. It reports an unbound register. Checking each path a query reads against the event's fields waits for §6.

### 13. Trace and provenance

*Partly built.* A query produces a normal engine trace. A value read through a policy names the register and the article in its provenance, for example `{source: lexostatus, lexostatus: voorschot, register: fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen#kroniek, article: fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen#1}`. The lexostatus is the article's `endpoint`. The result lists the chronolexograms read. A trace node per visited chronolexogram waits for §6.

### 14. Where it runs

The runtime that serves the register runs the keeper's query, over that register only. For a register bound to a cell, that is the cell. A cell still never works across cells.

## Why

### Benefits

- One rule language. A query is type-checked, traced and tested like any article, and a jurist can read it.
- Each part of a source sits with a text or an owner. The text test shows what has neither.
- Law and policy name no system, so a register can move from a cell to an API without changing them.
- No separate reduction language (about 2,300 lines of Rust and 600 of schema in the proof of concept).

### Tradeoffs

- The YAML is about 1.5 to 2 times longer. Until §7 lands, "latest" takes two steps.
- An engine run is slower. That is fine for a proof of concept. A production register needs §11 first.
- A proof of concept holds policy nobody adopted. Only the marking of §4 shows that.

### Alternatives Considered

**A reduction language in cell configuration** (RFC-022 §4.1, the proof of concept). Fast and short, but a second language without type checking or trace, outside a jurist's review.

**The lexostatus as a regulation of the cell** (Experiment A). It works, but nobody owns it.

**`reads` in the consumer's article** (28 September). The consumer's law would then prescribe how another body's register is searched.

**`open_terms` on the general law** (Awir 24, Awb 4:95), implemented per body. The engine selects implementations only by municipality, province or water board code, so two national bodies would collide.

## Implementation in this pull request

Built in `packages/cel` (PR #1689): the register binding and data source (§1, `register.rs`), legal order with `sequence` (§7), ids, references and roots (§3, `chronicle.rs`), the checks of §12, the provenance of §13, and `fictief_beleid_kroniek_toeslagen` marked as an assumption (§4). Not built: §5, §6 and §8 to §11. [RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047) explains why inputs from the application need no reduction.

## Relation to the position paper

The paper puts reduction in the cell ("Reductie vindt altijd plaats ín de cel waar de betreffende chronolexogrammen zijn vastgelegd") and synthesis with the consumer ("Synthese gebeurt bij de afnemer, niet bij de bron"). It calls reduction "een afleidingsproces" and leaves its form open. Writing it in the law format, in the keeper's policy, is our own choice.

The paper says nothing about chronolexogram ids or references. §3 is ours. The creator of Chronolexografie agreed to it on 29 September 2026.

## Open Questions

1. **Details of the engine language.** An instant type, for a law that compares moments within a day. Grouping by distinct values.
2. **A `source` per element**, for a consumer that asks a different register per row.
3. **Is `register` a schema element?** Now the name is the second half of the binding key. A `register` block in the keeper's policy would state it there, at the cost of a schema change.
