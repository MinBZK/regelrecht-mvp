## Context

Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 1 lets the head of a school grant leave "op aanvraag". The rest comes from the Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb). Awb 4:2 says what an application holds and 4:13 when the decision is due; 7:11 decides an objection. Art. 13a names none of them.

The law format could not express this:

- [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) hooks filter on a decision's legal character, and an application is not a decision.
- [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) has an `AANVRAAG` stage, but a hook there fires only when a caller runs the decision from that stage.
- No article could take another article's decision again.

## Decision

The law format says what is submitted and what is decided on it. [RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046) covers how a cell records it, and [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures) shows the pieces together.

1. **`produces` is a list** when an article yields more than one thing: one item per `type`, a kind of submission (`AANVRAAG`, `BEZWAAR`) or a legal character. A submission lists its `parameters` and names the decision it asks for (`requests`). A decision lists its `outputs` and its `derived` data.

   Every output belongs to at most one item. One that belongs to none is allowed only beside a decision that lists its own outputs, and is not part of that decision. The engine computes an output only when something reads it ([RFC-043](/rfcs/rfc-043)).
2. **A hook can apply to a submission** (`applies_to.submission`), narrowed to one article with `established_by`. It takes no `stage` or `decision_type`, and names a legal character or a submission, not both.
3. **`produces.moment`** names the parameter that holds the moment the law counts from, with its `legal_basis`. The engine does not read it; a recording runtime does.
4. **Each item has its own stage.** The stage named after the submission kind fires the hooks on the submission and computes no decision output. The other stages compute the decision. Outside a procedure every hook fires, as before.
5. **A decision gets its inputs** from the submission, then policy (`implements`), then the authority's findings (`decided_per_case_by`), passed apart from the parameters. Policy wins over a finding; a finding no article asks for is an error. A cell's answer wins over policy and over a finding ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)).
6. **A decision can be taken again.** The type `decision` holds a decision value. `source.article` calls an article's decision, by reference or by value. The call uses the decision's own fields and findings, plus findings given anew.
7. **An objection is a second submission.** Awb 7:11 establishes it and decides on it by calling the contested decision anew. It is admissible when on time and not against a decision on objection (`taken_on`). It gives cause when an output differs; `derived` data does not count.

```yaml
# Leerplichtwet 1969, art. 13a
produces:
  - type: AANVRAAG
    requests: leerplichtwet_1969#13a
    parameters: [bsn_jongere, eerste_verlofdag, laatste_verlofdag]
  - type: BESCHIKKING
    outputs: [verlofdagen, verlof_raakt_eerste_twee_lesweken, verlof_verleend]
```

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as in the Awb. A change to Awb 4:2 reaches every application through one Awb version.
- One article holds application and decision, as the text of art. 13a does.
- Whether an objection is founded is not up to the runtime. Awb 7:11 calls the contested article again on the facts of that day.
- A runtime without a chronicle uses the same law.

### Tradeoffs

- Every run of art. 13a also runs Awb 4:2 and 4:13.
- A hook parameter the caller does not pass must be `required: false`.
- To keep application and decision apart, a runtime must run stages.

Open points are listed in [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **The application as the first stage of the decision** (RFC-008 as it stood). Its hooks do not fire on their own.
- **The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2`). The text does not say it, and forgetting it loses the Awb fields silently.
- **`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** An application is not a decision (Awb 1:3).
- **One object with `submission` and `legal_character`.** The engine then skipped outputs at AANVRAAG by a rule of its own.
- **An `id` per item and `decides_on`** (PR #1689). Names the law does not have, pointing the wrong way: an application asks for a decision. For the objection, Awb 6:4 with a link from 7:11 would likewise add a key that `requests` covers.
- **A separate article for the decision.** The text of art. 13a does not split.
- **Decision inputs as parameters.** They would become fields of the application.
- **A field per parameter saying who supplies it** (`origin`). That is process, not law.

## Deviations from earlier RFCs

Their text is not changed.

- [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007): a hook on a legal character also matches an item with that `type`.
- [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008): the application is an item of its own, not the first stage of the decision; AANVRAAG remains the stage where its hooks fire.
- [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022): a decretogram is also an item with `type: BESCHIKKING`.
