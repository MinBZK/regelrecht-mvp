## Context

A specific law says that someone may apply for a decision. The Algemene wet bestuursrecht (Awb) says what every application contains and what follows once it is received. For vakantieverlof:

- Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 1: the head of the school grants leave "op aanvraag" of the parents.
- Awb 4:2 lid 1: "De aanvraag wordt ondertekend en bevat ten minste: a. de naam en het adres van de aanvrager; b. de dagtekening; c. een aanduiding van de beschikking die wordt gevraagd."
- Awb 4:13: the decision is due within a reasonable period "na ontvangst van de aanvraag", at most eight weeks.
- Awb 7:11: on an admissible objection the decision is reconsidered, and revoked where that gives cause.

Article 13a does not mention the Awb, and it does not need to. The law format could not say any of this. [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) hooks filter on the legal character of a decision, and an application is no decision. [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) has an `AANVRAAG` stage, but a hook there fires only when a caller runs the decision from that stage. And no article could take another article's decision again.

## Decision

The law format says what is submitted and what is decided on it. How any of it is recorded is [RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046).

### 1. An article lists what it produces

An article that yields more than one thing writes `produces` as a list, one item per thing. Art. 13a establishes the application and decides on it:

```yaml
# Leerplichtwet 1969, art. 13a
produces:
  - type: AANVRAAG
    requests: leerplichtwet_1969#13a
    parameters: [bsn_jongere, eerste_verlofdag, laatste_verlofdag]
  - type: BESCHIKKING
    decision_type: TOEKENNING
    outputs: [verlofdagen, verlof_raakt_eerste_twee_lesweken, verlof_verleend]
```

- `type` is a kind of submission (`AANVRAAG`, `BEZWAAR`) or a legal character. One item per type.
- A submission lists the parameters it contains and names the article whose decision it asks for (`requests`, Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder c), also when that is the same article.
- A decision lists its `outputs`, and under `derived` the data that follows from it without being part of it, such as the last day a parking card is valid.
- Every output belongs to exactly one item. Beside a decision that lists its own outputs, an output may belong to none: it is then not part of the decision, and another article reads it through `source.regulation`. The engine computes only what is asked: for a decision its item's outputs and `derived` data, for a `source.regulation` read the output it names.

An article that produces one thing keeps the object form, with `legal_character` or `submission.kind`. The mechanics and the loader's checks are in [Law Format](/concepts/law-format#what-the-article-produces).

### 2. The Awb hooks onto a submission

A hook names a kind of submission in `applies_to.submission`, as it names a legal character for a decision. `established_by: <regulation>#<article>` narrows it to the submission of one article.

```yaml
# Awb 4:2
hooks:
  - hook_point: pre_actions
    applies_to:
      submission: AANVRAAG
```

A hook names a legal character or a submission, not both. A hook on a submission takes no `stage` or `decision_type`, and `established_by` names no paragraph. The fields of a submission are the parameters its item lists plus those of the hooks that fire on it.

### 3. The moment that counts

`produces.moment` names the parameter that holds the moment the law counts from, with the provision that says so:

```yaml
# Awb 4:13
produces:
  moment:
    parameter: datum_ontvangst
    legal_basis:   # bwb_id and url left out here
      law: Algemene wet bestuursrecht
      article: '4:13'
      paragraph: '1'
```

That parameter is no field of the submission. The engine does not read the key; it is there for a runtime that records the submission as a dated fact.

### 4. Each produced thing has its own stage

Run at one stage of its procedure, an article computes only what belongs to that stage. The stage named after the kind of submission fires the hooks on the submission. Every other stage computes the decision, with the hooks on the decision at the stages they name.

| Stage | Art. 13a |
|---|---|
| AANVRAAG | Awb 4:2 and 4:13; no output of art. 13a |
| BESLUIT | the test of art. 13a, Awb 3:46 and 6:7 |
| BEKENDMAKING | Awb 6:8 |

An article in the object form computes every output at every stage; if it has both `submission` and a legal character, the hooks on the submission fire only at the stage named after the kind, and a procedure without that stage is an error. Run outside a procedure (a BDD scenario, the editor), the article fires every hook, as before.

### 5. Where a decision gets what it needs

The law says what a decision needs. Who supplies it is process, in this order:

1. **The submission.** A field it holds, by name.
2. **Policy.** An open term a regulation fills with `implements`.
3. **The deciding authority.** An open term with `decided_per_case_by`. Its judgement comes in as a finding, keyed by the term's id and apart from the parameters, and applies to every article in the execution that leaves the term to it.

A filling regulation wins over a finding, and the trace says it set the finding aside. A regulation that returns null is silent, and the finding applies. A finding that no article asks for is an error that names it. [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) puts one answer before all three: that of a cell.

### 6. A decision can be taken again

A parameter or input of type `decision` holds a decision value: `grondslag`, `taken_on`, `fields`, `findings` and `outputs` (only those of the decision item). `source.article` calls the decision of an article, named by reference or by a decision value. Called with a decision value, the engine runs its `grondslag` with its own fields and findings; findings given anew from the top win over them. The keys and the checks are in [Cross-Law References](/concepts/cross-law-references#calling-the-decision-of-an-article).

### 7. The objection is a second submission

Awb 7:11 establishes the objection and decides on it, as art. 13a does for the application. The objection holds the contested decision; the decision on it calls that decision anew:

```yaml
# Awb 7:11
produces:
  - type: BEZWAAR
    requests: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11
    parameters: [bestreden_besluit]
  - type: BESCHIKKING
    outputs: [bezwaar_ontvankelijk, heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding,
              bestreden_besluit_herroepen, nieuw_besluit]
input:
  - name: heroverweging
    type: decision
    nullable: true
    source:
      article: $bestreden_besluit
```

- **Admissible**: on time (Awb 6:9), and the contested decision was not taken on an objection (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a). `taken_on` tells that apart: both the decision on an objection and the new decision it takes have `taken_on: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11`.
- **Cause**: an output of the decision item comes out different. `derived` data does not count, so a later end date of a card alone gives none.
- **New decision**: on revocation `nieuw_besluit` holds the decision the call gave (lid 2).

The heroverweging runs only for an admissible objection, because the engine reads an input only when an action needs it ([RFC-043](/rfcs/rfc-043)).

## Why

### Benefits

- The application exists on its own, as in the Awb. No specific law names the Awb, and a change to Awb 4:2 reaches every application through one new Awb version.
- One article holds the application and the decision, as the text of art. 13a does.
- "Gegrond" is no choice of a runtime: it is art. 13a on the facts of that day, called by Awb 7:11.
- Nothing here is about recording. A runtime without a chronicle uses the same law.

### Tradeoffs

- More hooks fire: every run of art. 13a also runs Awb 4:2 and 4:13.
- A hook parameter that the caller does not pass must be `required: false`, or the specific law fails.
- A runtime that wants the application and the decision apart must run stages; a plain run fires everything at once.

What is not built or not modeled is on [Applications, Decisions and Objections](/concepts/administrative-procedures#open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **The application as the first stage of the decision** (RFC-008 as it stood). The hooks never fire.
- **The specific law names the Awb** (`includes: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:2`). The text does not say it, and a law that forgets it loses the Awb fields without notice.
- **`legal_character: AANVRAAG`.** An application is not a decision, and the legal character is decision vocabulary (Awb 1:3).
- **One object with both `submission` and `legal_character`.** The engine then skipped the article's outputs at AANVRAAG by a rule of its own; the list says it in the law.
- **An `id` per item, and `decides_on` from the decision to the application** (PR #1689). The ids are names the law does not have, and the law points the other way: an application asks for a decision. For the objection, Awb 6:4 establishing it with 7:11 linking to it would add a key `requests` already covers.
- **A separate article for the decision.** Art. 13a has one machine-readable block, and the text does not split.
- **Decision inputs as parameters of the article.** They would become fields of the application, and the applicant does not supply them.
- **A field per parameter that says who supplies it** (`origin`). Who supplies a value is process, not law.

## Deviations from earlier RFCs

Three accepted or draft RFCs are read differently here; their text is not changed.

- [RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007) matches a hook on `produces.legal_character`. A hook now also matches an item whose `type` is that legal character, and `applies_to.submission` an item whose `type` is that kind.
- [RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008) has the application as the first stage of the beschikking. Here it is an item of its own that names the decision with `requests`; the stage AANVRAAG remains where its hooks fire.
- [RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) calls a decretogram an output with `produces.legal_character: BESCHIKKING`. It is also one of an item with `type: BESCHIKKING`.

## Implementation

| Schema | Adds |
|---|---|
| v0.8.0 | `produces.submission`, `produces.moment`, `applies_to.submission`, `established_by` |
| v0.8.1 | `produces` as a list of items with `type`, `requests`, `parameters` and `outputs` |
| v0.8.3 | `BEZWAAR` as a kind of submission |
| v0.8.4 | the type `decision`, `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, `derived` |

The law model reads both forms of `produces`, and since the synthesis work the loader accepts an output beside a decision that belongs to no item (`load_check::reject_ambiguous_produces`); the engine keeps hooks on a submission in its hooks index, runs one stage with `LawExecutionService::execute_stage_at`, and calls an article in `resolve_article_call`. In the corpus: Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a, Awb 4:2, 4:13, 3:46, 6:5, 6:7 to 6:9, 7:10 and 7:11, BABW art. 49, each with scenarios next to the rulework.
