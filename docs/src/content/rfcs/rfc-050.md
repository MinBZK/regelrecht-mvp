## Decision

An objection (*bezwaar*) is a submission, like the application. The decision on it is a beschikking. Awb 7:11 plays the part that art. 13a plays for the application, and holds the procedure in rulework. The cell records and decides an objection like any other submission, and knows no Awb name.

In a procedure, the article that decides gets the earlier steps as context: the submission and the decision it refers to, with their fields and findings. When 7:11 calls that decision anew (`source.article: $bestreden_besluit`), the call uses the decision's own fields and findings. New findings come in from the top, and an article in the call must ask for them.

1. **Awb 7:11 establishes the objection and decides on it.** `produces` lists a `BEZWAAR` that `requests` the decision of 7:11 and holds `bestreden_besluit`, and a `BESCHIKKING` with the outputs `bezwaar_ontvankelijk`, `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding`, `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` and `nieuw_besluit` ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)).
2. **The Awb hooks onto every objection.** 6:5 asks what the bezwaarschrift contains. 6:9 makes the receipt the moment that counts and says whether it came in on time. 7:10 gives the last day to decide.
3. **The heroverweging is the contested decision taken anew.** 7:11 calls the contested decision anew, on the day of the decision on the objection (ex nunc). The call runs the decision's article with the decision's own fields and findings, plus what the deciding authority finds anew. It gives cause when an outcome of that article's decision item comes out different.
4. **A revoked decision stays.** 7:11 gives the new decision as `nieuw_besluit`, and the cell records it in the same append. The revoked one stays in the chronicle as it was. A reduction reads the decisions under 7:11 and leaves out what one of them revoked.
5. **Schema v0.8.3 adds `BEZWAAR`** as a kind of submission. Schema v0.8.4 adds what 7:11 needs to call an article named in data: `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, the field type `decision` and `derived` on the decision item.

### Awb 7:11 in rulework

The contested decision reaches 7:11 as a decision value:

| Key | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `grondslag` | The article the decision was taken under. |
| `taken_on` | The article of the submission the decision was taken on. The cell derives it from the chronolexogram the decision refers to, and stores nothing new. |
| `fields` | The parameters the article declares. |
| `findings` | What the deciding authority found for the case: the open terms of the article with `decided_per_case_by`, under their ids. For art. 13a: `specifieke_aard_beroep_ouder`, `verlof_valt_buiten_schoolvakanties` and `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar`. A term that a regulation or the cell filled is left out: the call anew asks it again of the regulation in force that day, or of the cell. |
| `outputs` | Only the outputs of the decision item of the article. |

`taken_on` tells a decision taken on objection apart. A decision on an application under art. 13a has `taken_on: leerplichtwet_1969#13a`. The decision on an objection and the new decision it takes both have `taken_on: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11`, although the new decision's `grondslag` is art. 13a. A value that an article gives through `source.article`, such as the heroverweging, has no `taken_on`: the engine builds it from a call, not from a submission.

Schema v0.8.4 describes `taken_on` as an optional key of a value built from a recorded decision. The engine narrows that for a parameter. A decision given as a parameter at the top of an execution, as `bestreden_besluit` is in a scenario, holds all five keys and no other, and its `grondslag` and `taken_on` are article references. Before the article runs, the engine refuses a value other than null or unknown that is no object, lacks a key, holds another or has no article reference in one of those two. The error names the parameter and the key. Without that rule a hand-written value without `taken_on` would pass wherever 7:11 does not read the key, and fail without naming the parameter where it does. A `taken_on` of `null` would silently make an objection against a decision on objection admissible. An input filled by `source.article` keeps its shape without `taken_on`, and one article may pass it to another as a parameter. A `taken_on` that such a parameter does hold must be an article reference too, at any depth.

7:11 itself uses these names:

| Name | Kind | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `bestreden_besluit` | parameter, type `decision` | The contested decision (lid 1 and 2, and Awb 6:5 lid 1 onder c). The objection holds it. |
| `bezwaarschrift_tijdig_ingediend` | parameter | What 6:9 gives on the objection. |
| `heroverweging` | input, type `decision`, nullable | `source.article: $bestreden_besluit`: the contested decision called anew, with its own fields and findings. The execution passes what the deciding authority finds anew separately from the parameters (`decide(..., findings, ...)`); a finding that no article in the call asks, or of the wrong type, is an error that names it. |
| `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` | output | On time, and the contested decision was not taken on an objection: `NOT EQUALS($bestreden_besluit.taken_on, "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11")` (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a). That covers the decision on an objection and the new decision it took. |
| `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding` | output, nullable | `IF bezwaar_ontvankelijk THEN NOT EQUALS($heroverweging.outputs, $bestreden_besluit.outputs) ELSE null`. Every output of the decision item counts, not only the grant: when `verlofdagen` changes and art. 13a refuses again, the refusal is revoked and another refusal takes its place. |
| `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | output | Whether the heroverweging gives cause. |
| `nieuw_besluit` | output, type `decision`, nullable | `IF bestreden_besluit_herroepen THEN $heroverweging ELSE null` (lid 2, "voor zover nodig een nieuw besluit"). |

The engine reads an input only when an action needs it (RFC-043), and `IF` reads only the branch it takes. So for an objection that is not admissible the heroverweging does not run, and `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding` stays `null`: there is no heroverweging (lid 1). A test in `packages/cel/tests/vakantieverlof.rs` checks that through the trace.

Only what the decision item names under `outputs` counts as the outcome. Data it lists under `derived`, such as the end of a validity, is recorded with the decision but plays no part in "aanleiding".

### What the cell does

The cell has one `record_submission` and one `decide`. It follows six rules. None of them names an Awb article.

| Rule | What the cell does | Why | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| A reference fills the decision field | `record_submission` with `refers_to: D` fills the field of type `decision` with D. Fields that D already holds are taken from D. | Earlier steps of a procedure are context. | The objection gets `bezwaartermijn_einddatum` from the decision. |
| Findings come in separately | `decide` takes the authority's findings apart from other input. A finding that no article asks for is an error. | A misspelled finding must fail, not vanish. | The head's new judgement on the profession. |
| A decision output becomes a decision | Each output of type `decision` that is not null is recorded as its own chronolexogram, with its own period. | Revoking and replacing land together or not at all. | `nieuw_besluit` from 7:11. |
| A decision is replaced once | A second replacement of the same decision is refused. | Two live replacements would contradict each other. This is our own guard, not an Awb rule. | A second objection against the same refusal. |
| The contested decision does not count against itself | During the reconsideration, reductions read the chronicle without the contested decision. | That decision is the one under review. | Leave granted earlier in the school year. |
| Answers are taken anew | The cell answers the engine for the whole decision, on its day, also in the article called anew; it runs its reduction there on its chronicle. Stage dates are those of the day as well. | The reconsideration looks at the facts of that day. | `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar` on the day of the decision on the objection. |

The chronolexogram keeps the contested decision by its id, and the head's findings among the decision's fields. Which findings the decision used is not stored. A finding that a regulation set aside was not used, even where the regulation gives the same value. The engine reports who filled each term: it lists under `open_terms` each open term that a finding, a regulation or the cell filled. An entry names the article that declares the term and, for a regulation, the article that implements it; for the cell, the reduction with its version and how far it read the chronicle ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048), §3). The cell gets that report by replaying the decision (`Cell::findings`, `Cell::explain`). The replay runs on the decision's own day, under the law in force then, with the decision's fields. The cell answers from the chronicle as it stood before the decision, so it gives the same lexostatus as then.

A replay whose outcome differs from the chronolexogram is an error. A cell is always opened with its reductions, because a replay without them would report a reduction's answer as a finding of the head ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048), §3).

A replay rests on one assumption: every source a decision reads can say what it knew on a given day. The chronicle and the law in force on a day can. A source that cannot, such as an external register without history, would need a facade. The facade records every value fetched with its moment, and a replay reads the recorded value. No such source is in the vakantieverlof case, and the facade is not built.

The school's reduction reads the decisions under 7:11 as a second input and leaves out the decisions they revoked.

| Case | Art. 13a anew | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Second leave in a school year | Refused again | Ongegrond, the decision stands |
| Profession judged anew on the employer's statement | Granted | Gegrond, revoked, leave granted |
| Refused anew on another ground | Refused again | Ongegrond: the outcomes do not name the ground |
| Late objection | Not run | Not admissible, the decision stands |
| Objection against the decision on an objection | Not run | Not admissible (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a) |
| Objection against the new decision taken on objection | Not run | Not admissible: its `taken_on` is 7:11 |
| Second objection against a decision already replaced | Would replace it again | The cell refuses the decision |

Not modeled: 6:6 and 6:11, 6:9 lid 2, the hearing (7:2), 7:10 lid 2 to 4, 7:12 lid 2 to 4.

### What moved out of the cell

The first version of this RFC put the procedure of Awb 7:11 in the cell (`packages/cel/src/objection.rs`). That procedure is now in rulework. The table below compares the first version with now. The cell still does two things. During the call, it answers the open term its reduction fills, from the chronicle without the contested decision, where the article called anew reads it (row "Reduction in the nested call"). After the call, it refuses a decision that would replace the contested decision a second time (row "Objection against a revoked decision").

| | First version | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Recording | `record_objection` next to `record_submission` | One `record_submission`, with `refers_to`. |
| Deciding | `decide_on_objection` next to `decide` | One `decide`. It runs the deciding article and records what that article produces. |
| Admissibility first | Order in Rust | 7:11 itself: the heroverweging is only read when `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` holds. |
| Heroverweging | The cell reruns the contested article | 7:11 calls the contested decision anew, with its own fields and findings and what is found anew. |
| "Aanleiding" | The cell compares outputs | 7:11 compares the outputs of the call with those of the contested decision. |
| Revoke and replace | The cell appends two chronolexograms | 7:11 gives the new decision as an output; the cell records both. |
| Awb names in the cell | `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | None. The reduction reads the revocation by that name, in YAML. |
| Reduction in the nested call | The cell ran art. 13a anew with its reductions bound | The reduction names an open term of art. 13a with `implements` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048), schema v0.8.5). The engine asks the cell for it during the call, and the trace of the decision on the objection (`Cell::explain`) shows the answer as one node; the cell's reasoning comes from `Cell::explain_answer`. Before 2026-10-16 the cell refreshed the fields of the decision value it passed; from 2026-10-16 to 2026-10-17 the engine ran the reduction itself. |
| A decision taken on objection | Refused by the cell | 7:11 reads `taken_on` of the contested decision, so it recognizes both the decision on an objection and the new decision it took. |
| Objection against a revoked decision | Refused by the cell | It is recorded and reconsidered. When the heroverweging would replace the decision again, the cell refuses the decision. This is our own guard: under the Awb a second objection has no object left (usually no interest), and art. 6:19 carries a pending objection over to the replacing decision. Open: 7:11 itself declares it not admissible, once the decision value says that the decision was replaced. |
| A finding anew on an objection that is not admissible | Not possible | There is no heroverweging, so no article asks the finding, and the engine refuses it. |

The deciding authority may give a finding for an article that the decision calls anew. That finding is recorded among the fields of the decision on the objection. When that decision is called anew in turn, it does not pass the finding on: the article it calls anew takes the finding of the contested decision again. Beroep is not modeled, so nothing calls a decision on an objection anew yet.

The call has two more limits. A decision value holds a decision among its fields as an id. A call that merges those fields therefore passes an id where the article expects a decision. The call also passes only the parameters the article itself declares and its findings per case. A value that only a hook on the decision or a stage of its procedure asks for does not reach it.

## Why

### Benefits

- "Gegrond" is no choice of the cell. It is art. 13a on the facts of that day, called by 7:11.
- The cell knows no Awb. Another article that decides on a submission referring to a decision works the same way.
- The reduction gives the whole answer. Whether a revoked decision counts is its rule.

### Tradeoffs

- "Differs in an outcome" is our reading of "aanleiding geeft". A heroverweging that refuses on another ground gives no cause: the outputs of art. 13a do not name the ground.
- Which article 7:11 calls is known only when it runs. The dependency graph and the editor do not see that edge, and the type checker knows the result only as a decision value.
- What is found anew is not in 7:11: it comes in from the top, apart from the parameters. A form for the deciding authority builds on `$bestreden_besluit.findings`.
- The new decision gets a period under Awb 6:7 and 6:8, recorded as `bezwaartermijn_einddatum`. For a decision taken on objection that is the period for appeal (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a); the name does not say so.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Awb 6:4 establishes the objection, 7:11 links to it** (`decides_on`, PR #1689). That adds a key; `requests` already does it.
- **The cell leaves revoked decisions out.** Then the reduction would not give the whole answer.
- **The cell runs 7:11** (the first version of this RFC). It needed `record_objection`, `decide_on_objection` and one Awb name in the cell.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.3: `BEZWAAR` in `produces.submission.kind`, as a list item's `type` and in `applies_to.submission`.
- Schema v0.8.4: `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, the field type `decision` and `derived` on the decision item of `produces`. The engine calls the article (`resolve_article_call` in `packages/engine/src/service.rs`), checks a fixed target at load (T5 in `typecheck.rs`), calls a decision value anew and takes findings apart from the parameters (`evaluate_law_with_findings`), reports per open term who filled it (`open_terms`), and compares lists and records strictly in `EQUALS`: an integer against a decimal of the same value is an error.
- Corpus: Awb 6:4, 6:5, 6:9, 7:2, 7:10 to 7:12 with `bezwaar.feature`, which passes the contested decision as a JSON object and what is found anew as findings; the school's reduction reads the decisions under 7:11.
- `packages/cel`: `record_submission` with `refers_to`, `decide` with findings and decision outputs, rows with `id`. The chronicle stores a decimal without loss ([#1732](https://github.com/NederlandseDigitaleDienst/regelrecht/pull/1732)), so a decision value read back and called anew compares equal to its stored outputs.
- Not built: beroep, the hearing, third parties, partial revocation, and replacing a decision a second time (the cell refuses it).

## Open questions

- **A lexostatus addressed by the term that asks for it.** A reduction answers one open term, named by the law and article that declare it (`implements` on art. 13a, `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar`). That suits one school answering one law. It does not scale to a cell that many laws ask the same thing. The Kamer van Koophandel would give the type of registration of an organization as one lexostatus, and every law that reads it declares its own open term, so the KvK cell would need an `implements` entry per asking law. Another address is open: a lexostatus the cell names itself, which an open term in the law points to. That address must not make the law name the cell; the law defines the interface, and the cell decides how it answers (2026-10-17).

- **A second answerer.** For the school holidays the school answers where it is established, from its own executogram ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). A DUO cell with the register of institutions (RIO) could answer that instead. That needs more than one answerer in an execution and a rule for which one answers a term. Not built (2026-10-18).
