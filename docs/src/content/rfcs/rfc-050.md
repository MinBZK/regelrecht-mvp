## Decision

An objection (*bezwaar*) is a submission, like the application. The decision on it is a beschikking. Awb 7:11 plays the part that art. 13a plays for the application, and it holds the whole procedure: the cell records and decides an objection as any other submission, and knows no Awb name.

1. **Awb 7:11 establishes the objection and decides on it.** `produces` lists a `BEZWAAR` that `requests` the decision of 7:11 and holds `bestreden_besluit`, and a `BESCHIKKING` with the outputs `bezwaar_ontvankelijk`, `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding`, `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` and `nieuw_besluit` ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)).
2. **The Awb hooks onto every objection.** 6:5 asks what the bezwaarschrift contains. 6:9 makes the receipt the moment that counts and says whether it came in on time. 7:10 gives the last day to decide.
3. **The heroverweging is the contested decision taken anew.** 7:11 calls the article the contested decision was taken under, with that decision's fields and what the deciding authority judges anew, on the day of the decision on the objection (ex nunc). It gives cause when an outcome of that article's decision item comes out different.
4. **A revoked decision stays.** 7:11 gives the new decision as `nieuw_besluit`, and the cell records it in the same append. The revoked one stays in the chronicle as it was. A reduction reads the decisions under 7:11 and leaves out what one of them revoked.
5. **Schema v0.8.3 adds `BEZWAAR`** as a kind of submission. Schema v0.8.4 adds what 7:11 needs to call an article named in data: `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, the field type `decision` and `derived` on the decision item.

### Awb 7:11 in rulework

The contested decision reaches 7:11 as a decision value `{grondslag, taken_on, fields, outputs}`. `outputs` holds only the outputs of the decision item of its article. `taken_on` is the article of the submission the decision was taken on: the cell derives it from the chronolexogram the decision refers to and stores nothing new. A decision on an application under art. 13a has `taken_on: leerplichtwet_1969#13a`. The decision on an objection and the new decision it takes have `taken_on: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11`, although the new decision's `grondslag` is art. 13a. A value that an article gives through `source.article`, such as the heroverweging, has no `taken_on`: the engine builds it from a call, not from a submission. Schema v0.8.4 describes the value as `{grondslag, fields, outputs}`; `taken_on` is an optional key the cell adds and the engine does not check.

| Name | Kind | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `bestreden_besluit` | parameter, type `decision` | The contested decision (lid 1 and 2, and Awb 6:5 lid 1 onder c). The objection holds it. |
| `bezwaarschrift_tijdig_ingediend` | parameter | What 6:9 gives on the objection. |
| `heroverweging_oordelen` | parameter, type `object`, nullable | What the deciding authority judges anew, under the names the contested decision's article uses. An open term cannot be an object, so it is a parameter the objection does not hold. |
| `heroverweging` | input, type `decision` | `source.article: $bestreden_besluit.grondslag`, with `parameters_from: [$bestreden_besluit.fields, $heroverweging_oordelen]`. |
| `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` | output | On time, and the contested decision was not taken on an objection: `NOT EQUALS($bestreden_besluit.taken_on, "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11")` (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a). That covers the decision on an objection and the new decision it took. |
| `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding` | output, nullable | `IF bezwaar_ontvankelijk THEN NOT EQUALS($heroverweging.outputs, $bestreden_besluit.outputs) ELSE null`. Every output of the decision item counts, not only the grant: when `verlofdagen` changes and art. 13a refuses again, the refusal is revoked and another refusal takes its place. |
| `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | output | Whether the heroverweging gives cause. |
| `nieuw_besluit` | output, type `decision`, nullable | `IF bestreden_besluit_herroepen THEN $heroverweging ELSE null` (lid 2, "voor zover nodig een nieuw besluit"). |

The engine reads an input only when an action needs it (RFC-043), and `IF` reads only the branch it takes. So for an objection that is not admissible the heroverweging does not run, and `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding` stays `null`: there is no heroverweging (lid 1). A test in `packages/cel/tests/vakantieverlof.rs` checks that through the trace.

Only what the decision item names under `outputs` counts as the outcome. Data it lists under `derived`, such as the end of a validity, is recorded with the decision but plays no part in "aanleiding".

### What the cell does

The cell has one `record_submission` and one `decide`. It applies six general rules, none of them about the Awb.

- **A reference fills the decision field.** `record_submission` takes an optional `refers_to`, a decision in the chronicle. A field of type `decision` (at most one per submission) holds that decision, and the cell fills every other field the decision holds by name, such as `bezwaartermijn_einddatum` (Awb 6:8). The submitter supplies neither, and a value taken by name must fit the field, or the cell refuses it as a setup error that names the field and both articles. The chronolexogram keeps the decision by its id; the engine gets its decision value. Its `findings` hold only the deciding article's own terms whose value the chronolexogram names as a finding the decision used (its `findings` list). A term a regulation filled in the authority's place is in neither part: the regulation decided it, and a call anew reads the regulation in force on its own day. A finding given for an article the decision called is recorded with the decision that article produced, the new decision, and the value leaves it out.
- **Findings go apart.** `decide` takes the deciding authority's findings apart from what it supplies, and all of them go to the engine apart from the parameters, so a finding applies to every article in the decision that leaves the term to the authority, also when the deciding article and the article called anew name the term the same. The authority gives a finding for a call anew only once that call happens, so the app asks for it after admissibility; given while the decision does not call anew, no article asks it and the cell refuses the decision. The chronolexogram names the findings the decision used; a finding a regulation set aside is not among them.
- **A decision output is a decision.** `decide` records every output of type `decision` that is not null as a chronolexogram of its own, under the value's `grondslag` and referring to the same submission. It runs the stages of that article again with the value's `fields`, so a hook gives the new decision its own outcome (its own period under Awb 6:7 and 6:8; for a decision taken on objection that is the period for appeal), and checks that the outputs of its decision item are the value's. They land in the chronicle together, or none of them.
- **A decision is replaced once.** The cell refuses a `decide` that would record a decision output in the place of decision D when the chronicle already holds one: a decision taken on a submission that refers to D, held by id by the decision on that submission. Nothing is appended.
- **A replacing decision does not count against itself.** When the deciding article has an output of type `decision`, the reductions read the chronicle without the decision the submission refers to. The value of that decision the cell gives the deciding article is always answered again without it, because it is the decision being answered again.
- **The reduction's answer reaches the call.** The cell answers again, on the day of the decision, the fields of the decision value that its reductions answer for the article of that decision (here `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar`). A date a stage of that article requires of the cell is the day of the decision too, as in the new decision the cell records. The chronolexogram keeps the contested decision by id, not those answers.

A row for a reduction carries the decision's fields, its outputs and its `id`, and nothing the cell adds. The school's reduction has a second input, `source: {decisions: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11}`. It collects the ids of the decisions that a decision on objection revoked and leaves those out (`NOT` with `IN`). A `FOREACH` body does not see the binding of an outer `FOREACH`, so the link is a list of ids, not a nested loop.

| Case | Art. 13a anew | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Second leave in a school year | Refused again | Ongegrond, the decision stands |
| Profession judged anew on the employer's statement | Granted | Gegrond, revoked, leave granted |
| Refused anew on another ground | Refused again | Ongegrond: the outcomes do not name the ground |
| Late objection | Not run | Not admissible, the decision stands |
| Objection against the decision on an objection | Not run | Not admissible (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a) |
| Objection against the new decision taken on objection | Not run | Not admissible: its `taken_on` is 7:11 |
| Second objection against a decision already replaced | Would replace it again | The cell refuses the decision |

Not modeled: 6:6 and 6:11, 6:9 lid 2, the hearing (7:2), 7:10 lid 2 to 4, 7:12 lid 2 to 4.

### Where this should end up

The first version of this RFC put the procedure of Awb 7:11 in the cell (`packages/cel/src/objection.rs`). That has moved into rulework.

| | First version | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Recording | `record_objection` next to `record_submission` | One `record_submission`, with `refers_to`. Done. |
| Deciding | `decide_on_objection` next to `decide` | One `decide`. It runs the deciding article and records what that article produces. Done. |
| Admissibility first | Order in Rust | 7:11 itself: the heroverweging is only read when `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` holds. Done. |
| Heroverweging | The cell reruns the contested article | 7:11 calls the article of the contested decision, with that decision's fields and what is judged anew. Done. |
| "Aanleiding" | The cell compares outputs | 7:11 compares the outputs of the call with those of the contested decision. Done. |
| Revoke and replace | The cell appends two chronolexograms | 7:11 gives the new decision as an output; the cell records both. Done. |
| Awb names in the cell | `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | None. The reduction reads the revocation by that name, in YAML. Done. |
| Reduction in the nested call | The cell ran art. 13a anew with its reductions bound | The cell refreshes the fields of the decision value it passes. Open: a reduction that fills an open term of art. 13a with `implements`, so the engine finds it during the call. |
| A decision taken on objection | Refused by the cell | 7:11 reads `taken_on` of the contested decision, so it recognizes both the decision on an objection and the new decision it took. Done. |
| Objection against a revoked decision | Refused by the cell | It is recorded and reconsidered. When the heroverweging would replace the decision again, the cell refuses the decision (a general rule, no Awb name). Done. |
| Judgement anew on an objection that is not admissible | Not possible | Open: the decision keeps `heroverweging_oordelen` as the deciding authority supplied it, but there is no heroverweging, so nothing reads it. A general rule that refuses supplied values the decision never read is out of scope. |

Two further limits of the call. A decision value holds a decision among its fields as an id, so a call that merges those fields passes an id where the article asks a decision. The call passes only the parameters the article itself declares and its judgements per case, so a value that only a hook on the decision or a stage of its procedure asks for does not reach it.

## Why

### Benefits

- "Gegrond" is no choice of the cell. It is art. 13a on the facts of that day, called by 7:11.
- The cell knows no Awb. Another article that decides on a submission referring to a decision works the same way.
- The reduction gives the whole answer. Whether a revoked decision counts is its rule.

### Tradeoffs

- "Differs in an outcome" is our reading of "aanleiding geeft". A heroverweging that refuses on another ground gives no cause: the outputs of art. 13a do not name the ground.
- Which article 7:11 calls is known only when it runs. The dependency graph and the editor do not see that edge, and the type checker knows the result only as a decision value.
- What is judged anew is a parameter of type `object`, not an open term with `decided_per_case_by`: the schema allows no object for an open term.
- The new decision gets a period under Awb 6:7 and 6:8, recorded as `bezwaartermijn_einddatum`. For a decision taken on objection that is the period for appeal (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a); the name does not say so.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Awb 6:4 establishes the objection, 7:11 links to it** (`decides_on`, PR #1689). That adds a key; `requests` already does it.
- **The cell leaves revoked decisions out.** Then the reduction would not give the whole answer.
- **The cell runs 7:11** (the first version of this RFC). It needed `record_objection`, `decide_on_objection` and one Awb name in the cell.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.3: `BEZWAAR` in `produces.submission.kind`, as a list item's `type` and in `applies_to.submission`.
- Schema v0.8.4: `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, the field type `decision` and `derived` on the decision item of `produces`. The engine calls the article (`resolve_article_call` in `packages/engine/src/service.rs`), checks a fixed target at load (T5 in `typecheck.rs`), calls a decision value anew and takes findings apart from the parameters (`evaluate_law_with_findings`), and compares lists and records strictly in `EQUALS`: an integer against a decimal of the same value is an error.
- Corpus: Awb 6:4, 6:5, 6:9, 7:2, 7:10 to 7:12 with `bezwaar.feature`, which passes the contested decision as a JSON object; the school's reduction reads the decisions under 7:11.
- `packages/cel`: `record_submission` with `refers_to`, `decide` with decision outputs, rows with `id`.
- Not built: beroep, the hearing, third parties, partial revocation, a second objection after a revocation.

## Open Questions

- The chronicle stores decimals as JSON floats, so a decimal output compared with its stored copy can differ; the chronicle should store them losslessly.
