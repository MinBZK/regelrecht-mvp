## Decision

An objection (*bezwaar*) is a submission, like the application. The decision on it is a beschikking. Awb 7:11 plays the part that art. 13a plays for the application.

1. **Awb 7:11 establishes the objection and decides on it.** `produces` lists a `BEZWAAR` that `requests` the decision of 7:11, and a `BESCHIKKING` with the outputs `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` and `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)).
2. **The Awb hooks onto every objection.** 6:5 asks what the bezwaarschrift contains. 6:9 makes the receipt the moment that counts and says whether it came in on time. 7:10 gives the last day to decide.
3. **The heroverweging is art. 13a anew.** The cell runs it again on the facts of the day (ex nunc). It gives cause when an output of art. 13a comes out different.
4. **A revoked decision stays, and says so.** The cell records the new decision in its place. The revoked one stays in the chronicle. Its row for a reduction carries `bestreden_besluit_herroepen: true`, and the reduction leaves it out.
5. **Schema v0.8.3 adds `BEZWAAR`** as a kind of submission. The law model reads it as one (`SUBMISSION_KINDS`); the engine is otherwise unchanged.

### Recording the objection

`Cell::record_objection` records a chronolexogram of kind `BEZWAAR` that refers to the contested decision.

- The fields are the parameters of the hooks. What a hook outputs is no field: 7:11 asks `bezwaarschrift_tijdig_ingediend`, and 6:9 gives it.
- `bezwaartermijn_einddatum` comes from the contested decision (Awb 6:8). The objector may not supply it.
- An objection does not change the application. Awb 7:11 lid 1 reconsiders the decision "op grondslag van het bezwaar", and 6:5 asks only for the grounds. A smaller request is a new application (`record_submission`).
- A late objection is recorded: whether it came in on time is what 6:9 computes, and 7:11 lid 1 then declares it not admissible. Excusable lateness (Awb 6:11) is not modeled.
- The cell refuses an objection against what is no decision, against a revoked decision, and against a decision taken on objection (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a: beroep is open).

### The decision on the objection

`Cell::decide_on_objection` takes up to four steps.

1. **Admissibility.** Awb 7:11 runs as if the heroverweging gives cause. If it still does not revoke, the objection is not admissible: there is no heroverweging (lid 1), and the decision records no judgement on it (`null`; the open term is not required).
2. **Heroverweging.** Only for an admissible objection. Art. 13a runs with the application's own fields, and the policy and reduction of the day. The head's judgements come from the contested decision, unless the head gives new ones. The reduction does not read the contested decision.
3. **Decision on the objection.** Awb 7:11 runs with whether the heroverweging differs. One chronolexogram records it.
4. **New decision.** When `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` is true, a chronolexogram under art. 13a records the heroverweging. It has its own period from Awb 6:8. Both refer to the objection, and the chronicle takes both or neither.

| Case | Art. 13a anew | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Second leave in a school year | Refused again | Ongegrond, the decision stands |
| Profession judged anew on the employer's statement | Granted | Gegrond, revoked, leave granted |
| Late objection | Not run | Not admissible, the decision stands |

Not modeled: 6:6 and 6:11, 6:9 lid 2, the hearing (7:2), 7:10 lid 2 to 4, 7:12 lid 2 to 4.

### Where this should end up

This RFC puts the procedure of Awb 7:11 in the cell. The end state has it in rulework, and the cell knows no Awb at all. None of the end state is implemented yet.

| | Now | End state |
|---|---|---|
| Recording | `record_objection` next to `record_submission` | One `record_submission`. Whether it is an application or an objection follows from the item in `produces`. |
| Deciding | `decide_on_objection` next to `decide` | One `decide`. It runs the deciding article and records what that article produces. |
| Admissibility first | Order in Rust | 7:11 itself: the heroverweging is only asked when `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` holds. |
| Heroverweging | The cell reruns the contested article | 7:11 calls the article of the contested decision, with that decision's fields and what is judged anew. |
| "Aanleiding" | The cell compares outputs | 7:11 compares the outputs of the call with those of the contested decision. |
| Revoke and replace | The cell appends two chronolexograms | 7:11 produces the decision on the objection, and the called article produces the new decision. The cell records both. |
| Awb names in the cell | `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | None. A reduction still reads the revocation by that name, but in YAML. |

What the engine needs for this:

- **Calling an article named in data.** Which article decided is the `grondslag` of the contested decision, a value in the chronicle. A `source` that takes its regulation and article from a value does not exist yet.
- **A decision given as input.** 7:11 must receive the contested decision's fields and outputs as an input, the way a reduction receives `decisions`.

When both exist, this RFC is updated to move the procedure into Awb 7:11, and `packages/cel/src/objection.rs` disappears. That is why it stays a draft.

## Why

### Benefits

- "Gegrond" is no choice of the cell. It is art. 13a on the facts of that day.
- The reduction gives the whole answer. Whether a revoked decision counts is its rule.

### Tradeoffs

- The procedure of Awb 7:11 lives in the cell, in Rust (`packages/cel/src/objection.rs`), not in rulework. The cell decides admissibility before the heroverweging (lid 1). It reruns the article of the contested decision on the facts of the day, and reads "aanleiding" as an output that differs. It revokes and replaces in one step (lid 2). In rulework, 7:11 would have to call the article of the contested decision, and which article that is only the chronicle knows (the `grondslag` of that decision). The engine cannot call an article named in data yet.
- Within that procedure the cell knows one Awb name, `bestreden_besluit_herroepen`, as it knows the stages BESLUIT and BEKENDMAKING. It finds the judgement it answers by shape: the one boolean term judged per case.
- "Differs in an output" is our reading of "aanleiding geeft". A heroverweging that refuses on another ground gives no cause: the outputs of art. 13a do not name the ground.
- A decision under 7:11 holds `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` itself. Its row for a reduction keeps that value, and no row says whether it was revoked: no objection lies against it (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a). No reduction reads those yet.
- The new decision gets an objection period, although only beroep is open against it.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Awb 6:4 establishes the objection, 7:11 links to it** (`decides_on`, PR #1689). That adds a key; `requests` already does it.
- **The cell leaves revoked decisions out.** Then the reduction would not give the whole answer.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.3: `BEZWAAR` in `produces.submission.kind`, as a list item's `type` and in `applies_to.submission`.
- Corpus: Awb 6:4, 6:5, 6:9, 7:2, 7:10 to 7:12 with `bezwaar.feature`; the reduction skips a revoked grant.
- `packages/cel`: `record_objection`, `decide_on_objection`, `Shape::given`, `reading::revoked_by`.
- Not built: beroep, the hearing, third parties, a second objection after a revocation.
