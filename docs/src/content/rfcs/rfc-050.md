## Context

A zorgtoeslag case runs for years: voorschot (Awir 16), monthly installments (Awir 22), a voorschot per following year (Awir 15 lid 5, 16 lid 2), the aanslag, the toekenning (Awir 19), and a nabetaling (Awir 24) or terugvordering (Awir 26, 26a, 28). Some steps depend on another body, and the money moves through a bank.

RFC-022 gives no rules for time. This RFC records the generic rules, with the source of each: the law, an RFC, or our own choice. The [Zorgtoeslag as Chronolexography](/components/zorgtoeslag-chronolexography) page applies them to the case.

## Decision

### 1. One clock, forward only

A runtime has one clock. In the demo it is the reference date, and everything is recorded on that day. The clock moves forward only.

- **A future fact is not a fact.** The cell refuses to execute a day after the current one. The voorschot does not record its future installments.
- **A chronicle refuses a chronolexogram recorded before its last one.** To go back, start again.
- **A preview is not a fact.** `preview_execution` and `preview_decision` execute the law without recording, to show the next moment.

Moving the clock several days at once passes every moment on the way. An installment of a skipped day holds from that day and reads the case as it was then.

The cell orders a chronicle by `effective_at`, then by recording moment, then by recording order ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) §7).

### 2. An execution is a rule per moment

Awir 22 does not give a list of payments. It says per month whether an installment falls: 12 installments, the first in December before the berekeningsjaar, then one each month (lid 1 to 4).

So Awir 22 is modeled as a rule per month. Each time the clock reaches a month, the cell asks the law. Only an installment that is executed becomes a chronolexogram. Awir 22 does not give the amount. A fictitious policy of the Dienst Toeslagen does, marked as such.

The policy article says which event it yields and when, with `produces.events` ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049) §5): `when` names the boolean output that says whether a chronolexogram arises. The law decides this, not the cell. The rest is how the holder records, so the stream event says it:

| Key in the stream | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `executed_on: {days, parameter, once_per: month}` | The policy article that gives the days, the parameter the day goes into, and at most once per month per case and period |
| `period: {parameter, unit}` | The parameter that gives the period the execution concerns. Optional |
| `until: {stage}` | No chronolexogram arises once the case has a chronolexogram of that stage (error kind `ended`) |
| `refers_to` (required) | The decision being executed, by stage or by article |

The days are a rule of the holder, so they sit in an article of the holder's policy, like the day of a decision (§3). The stream names that article per execution event (`executed_on.days`). Its one date output is the next day the holder executes, after a given day. Null means no day.

`Cell::due_executions` asks that article from the day after which the caller asks, until it gives no day or one after the end of the range. It asks per period, with what the case reads (`reads`) and with the previous day in the parameter of `executed_on`. A day that does not lie after the one asked is an error in the policy. The cell itself keeps only what concerns recording: the required reference, `until` and `once_per`.

In the fictitious policy of the Dienst Toeslagen, art. 5 gives the day of the voorschot and then the 1st of every month. Whether an order arises on such a day is for art. 1 to say.

### 3. When a decision is due

A stream may name, per decision event, an article whose one date output is the day the holder takes the decision (`decided_on`).

- **No day means not yet due.** A `null`, or a day that rests on a missing fact, means the decision is not due. The cell refuses a decision before its day or without one. A preview is not refused.
- **The decision that answers the application is the exception.** Whoever answers the application takes it when they do. This is the first decision stage after the application: in the Awir, the voorschot.
- **Executions get their days the same way.** The stream names the article (`executed_on.days`, §2). Deciding and executing then both follow the holder's policy, which a jurist can read.
- **The first period with a day is due.** `Cell::due_decision` checks each period of the case without that decision, from the one applied for. A period without a day does not hold up a later one.

In the zorgtoeslag case these days come from fictitious policies, marked as such.

### 4. A decision concerns one period and applies that period's law

The first decision of an event concerns the period the application asks for (its parameter with `rol: TIJDVAK`, [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)). The next one concerns the period after it. Awir 15 lid 5 makes this law: "Een aanvraag wordt geacht mede te zijn gedaan voor op het berekeningsjaar volgende berekeningsjaren." The cell refuses a period before the one applied for.

A decision applies the law in force on the first day of its period. A voorschot for 2026 decided in November 2025 uses the law of 2026, and its chronolexogram names that version (`regulation_valid_from`). This is what `unit: year` means. A law that means another day needs another unit. The Awb periods of the decision itself run from the day it is taken.

Everything attached to a decision runs per period: references, `once_per`, `until` and due days. So December 2025 pays the last installment of 2025 and the first of 2026. The toekenning over 2025 ends only the 2025 installments. A policy that reads the chronicle gets the period from the cell.

### 5. Decisions nobody applies for

A beschikking without `decides_on` is taken ex officio ([RFC-049](/rfcs/rfc-049) §2), such as the aanslag under AWR 11. It has no application to take two things from.

- **The period.** Schema v0.8.0 allows `rol: TIJDVAK` only with `waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE`. Nobody applies for an aanslag, so the stream names the parameter that gives the period (`period: {parameter: belastingjaar, unit: year}`). This is our own stopgap. The cell refuses it on a decision whose law names a period, or that is taken on a submission.
- **Whom it concerns.** The stream names the parameters that identify the subject (`subject: [bsn]`). The cell takes one such decision per period per subject.

`Cell::due_ex_officio` gives, per subject, the first period without such a decision that has a day. It checks from the first decision about that subject through the current period, so a skipped period is asked again. Without an earlier decision it starts at the period before the current one (our own choice). A stream may set `first_period`. More than 50 periods is an error.

### 6. Messages between cells

The law does not say who the bank is or how a message travels, so transport belongs to the runtime. In the demo, configured channels map a chronolexogram of one cell to an article of another, field by field, and set the reference of the answer.

A cell receives a message by executing the receiving article once, with the message as parameters. An event the article yields (`produces.events`) arises on receipt when its stream gives no `executed_on`. A receipt may read a policy of its holder, as the bank reads a balance before a debit.

A message may be offered more than once.

- **One answer per message.** A receipt's stream gives a required `refers_to` to the chronolexogram it answers, or `identified_by`, a parameter that identifies the message (the betaalkenmerk). A second answer is refused with error kind `answered`. A receipt with neither is refused when the cell derives its shape.
- **Not for this article.** An article that does not answer the referenced chronolexogram refuses with `not_addressed`. A sender counts neither error as a failure. A message no article takes stays undelivered.
- **The moment of a message.** It may not hold after it is recorded, nor before the chronolexogram it refers to.

### 7. A refusal is a decision

A decision that grants nothing is still a decision (Awb 1:3). So a following decision that refuses is recorded, and the next period can follow. Otherwise the cell would offer the same period forever.

The demo does not record a first decision that a caseworker refuses: it ends the case. That is the demo's case lifecycle, not a rule of the cell.

## Why

### Benefits

- Time comes from the law, not from the screen.
- A future installment is not recorded, so it cannot be wrong when the voorschot changes.
- Messages can be delivered again without doing anything twice.

### Tradeoffs

- A rule per month means the cell asks the law every month.
- `decided_on` depends on a policy article. Where the holder has none, the article is fictitious.
- The period and subject of a decision ex officio are in the stream, outside the law.
- Ordering by `effective_at` means a late fact about an earlier moment can change an earlier reading. That is intended: both moments are kept.

### Alternatives Considered

**The voorschot records its installments as a list** (the earlier proof of concept). Rejected: a future installment is not a fact, and a revised voorschot would leave a wrong list.

**The demo computes the next moment from its own calendar.** Rejected: the dates would come from the demo, and each law would need its own calendar.

**`decided_on` without a day means now.** Rejected: an unknown day would make a decision due before the law allows it.

**The law of the day the decision is taken.** Rejected: a voorschot for 2026 decided in November 2025 would use the law of 2025.

**The transport in the law or the policy.** Rejected: no text says who the bank is.

## Implementation in this pull request

All of it is built: in `packages/cel` (`Cell::decide`, `execute`, `receive`, `due_executions`, `due_decision`, `due_ex_officio`, `preview_decision`, `preview_execution`, the chronicle's order) and in `frontend-demo` (the clock, "To the next moment", channels and outbox). The zorgtoeslag case runs from application to terugvordering in `packages/cel/tests` and in the demo.

## Open Questions

1. **Announcement and objection of later decisions.** The following voorschot, the toekenning and the terugvordering have no announcement stage in the demo's case lifecycle.
2. **The moment of a late answer.** A message that arrives only on a retry holds from its arrival, not from the order it answers.
3. **The period of a decision ex officio in the law** (§5): a role for a period nobody applies for.
4. **Awir 19 lid 2.** Without an aanslag there is no toekenning in the demo. Awir 19 models the latest date (31 December of the following year), but the holder's policy only gives a day from an aanslag.
5. **Fixed dates of a future decision.** The demo finds them by comparing two previews in different months. That is a heuristic. The law should mark such a date itself.
6. **Not modeled:** entitlement per calendar month (Zorgtoeslagwet 2 lid 5), revision of a voorschot (Awir 16 lid 5, 17), interest and collection (Awir 27 to 29).
