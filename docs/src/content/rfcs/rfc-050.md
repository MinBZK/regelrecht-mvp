## Decision

An objection (*bezwaar*) is a submission, like the application. The decision on it is a beschikking. Awb 7:11 plays the part that art. 13a plays for the application. It holds the whole procedure. The cell records and decides an objection like any other submission, and knows no Awb name.

In a procedure, the earlier steps are context for the engine and the chronicle. The submission and the decision it refers to are context for the article that decides, with their fields and findings. When 7:11 calls that decision anew (`source.article: $bestreden_besluit`), the call uses the decision's own fields and findings. New findings come in from the top, and an article in the call must ask for them.

1. **Awb 7:11 establishes the objection and decides on it.** `produces` lists a `BEZWAAR` that `requests` the decision of 7:11 and holds `bestreden_besluit`, and a `BESCHIKKING` with the outputs `bezwaar_ontvankelijk`, `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding`, `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` and `nieuw_besluit` ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)).
2. **The Awb hooks onto every objection.** 6:5 asks what the bezwaarschrift contains. 6:9 makes the receipt the moment that counts and says whether it came in on time. 7:10 gives the last day to decide.
3. **The heroverweging is the contested decision taken anew.** 7:11 calls the contested decision anew, on the day of the decision on the objection (ex nunc). The call runs the decision's article with the decision's own fields and findings, plus what the deciding authority finds anew. It gives cause when an outcome of that article's decision item comes out different.
4. **A revoked decision stays.** 7:11 gives the new decision as `nieuw_besluit`, and the cell records it in the same append. The revoked one stays in the chronicle as it was. A reduction reads the decisions under 7:11 and leaves out what one of them revoked.
5. **Schema v0.8.3 adds `BEZWAAR`** as a kind of submission. Schema v0.8.4 adds what 7:11 needs to call an article named in data: `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, the field type `decision` and `derived` on the decision item.

### Awb 7:11 in rulework

The contested decision reaches 7:11 as a decision value:

| Key | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `grondslag` | The article the decision was taken under. |
| `taken_on` | The article of the submission the decision was taken on. The cell derives it from the chronolexogram the decision refers to, and stores nothing new. |
| `fields` | The parameters the article declares. |
| `findings` | What the deciding authority found for the case: the open terms of the article with `decided_per_case_by`, under their ids. For art. 13a: `specifieke_aard_beroep_ouder` and `verlof_valt_buiten_schoolvakanties`. |
| `outputs` | Only the outputs of the decision item of the article. |

`taken_on` tells a decision taken on objection apart. A decision on an application under art. 13a has `taken_on: leerplichtwet_1969#13a`. The decision on an objection and the new decision it takes both have `taken_on: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11`, although the new decision's `grondslag` is art. 13a. A value that an article gives through `source.article`, such as the heroverweging, has no `taken_on`: the engine builds it from a call, not from a submission. Schema v0.8.4 describes `taken_on` as an optional key of a value built from a recorded decision. The engine does not check it.

7:11 itself uses these names:

| Name | Kind | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `bestreden_besluit` | parameter, type `decision` | The contested decision (lid 1 and 2, and Awb 6:5 lid 1 onder c). The objection holds it. |
| `bezwaarschrift_tijdig_ingediend` | parameter | What 6:9 gives on the objection. |
| `heroverweging` | input, type `decision`, nullable | `source.article: $bestreden_besluit`: the contested decision called anew, with its own fields and findings. What the deciding authority finds anew, the execution gives apart from the parameters (`decide(..., findings, ...)`); a finding that no article in the call asks, or of the wrong type, is an error that names it. |
| `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` | output | On time, and the contested decision was not taken on an objection: `NOT EQUALS($bestreden_besluit.taken_on, "algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11")` (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a). That covers the decision on an objection and the new decision it took. |
| `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding` | output, nullable | `IF bezwaar_ontvankelijk THEN NOT EQUALS($heroverweging.outputs, $bestreden_besluit.outputs) ELSE null`. Every output of the decision item counts, not only the grant: when `verlofdagen` changes and art. 13a refuses again, the refusal is revoked and another refusal takes its place. |
| `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | output | Whether the heroverweging gives cause. |
| `nieuw_besluit` | output, type `decision`, nullable | `IF bestreden_besluit_herroepen THEN $heroverweging ELSE null` (lid 2, "voor zover nodig een nieuw besluit"). |

The engine reads an input only when an action needs it (RFC-043), and `IF` reads only the branch it takes. So for an objection that is not admissible the heroverweging does not run, and `heroverweging_geeft_aanleiding` stays `null`: there is no heroverweging (lid 1). A test in `packages/cel/tests/vakantieverlof.rs` checks that through the trace.

Only what the decision item names under `outputs` counts as the outcome. Data it lists under `derived`, such as the end of a validity, is recorded with the decision but plays no part in "aanleiding".

### What the cell does

The cell has one `record_submission` and one `decide`. It applies six general rules, none of them about the Awb.

- **Findings go apart.** `decide` takes the deciding authority's findings separately from what the authority supplies, and passes them to the engine separately from the parameters. A finding so applies to every article in the decision that leaves its term to the authority, also when the deciding article and the article called anew use the same name for the term. For a call anew, the authority gives its findings only once that call happens, so the app asks for them after admissibility. If the authority gives them while the decision does not call anew, no article asks for them: the engine refuses them, and the cell records no decision. The chronolexogram lists the findings the decision used. A finding that a regulation set aside is not on that list.
- **A reference fills the decision field.** `record_submission` takes an optional `refers_to`: the id of a decision in the chronicle. A field of type `decision` gets that id; a submission has at most one such field. Every other field that the decision holds under the same name gets the decision's value, such as `bezwaartermijn_einddatum` (Awb 6:8). The submitter supplies neither. A value taken by name must fit the field. If it does not, the cell refuses with a setup error that names the field and both articles. The chronolexogram keeps the decision by its id, and the engine gets its decision value. Its `findings` hold only the findings on the deciding article's own terms that the chronolexogram lists as used. Two kinds of value are left out. A term that a regulation filled in the authority's place is left out, because the regulation decided it, and a call anew reads the regulation in force on its own day. A finding given for an article that the decision called is left out too: it is recorded with the decision that article produced, the new decision.
- **A decision output is a decision.** `decide` records each output of type `decision` that is not null as a chronolexogram of its own. That chronolexogram is under the value's `grondslag` and refers to the same submission. The cell runs the stages of that article again with the value's `fields` and `findings`. A hook so gives the new decision its own outcome: its own period under Awb 6:7 and 6:8, which for a decision taken on objection is the period for appeal. The cell checks that the outputs of the decision item match the value. All chronolexograms of one call land in the chronicle together, or none do.
- **A decision is replaced once.** Decision D may already have a replacement in the chronicle: a decision taken on a submission that refers to D, held by id by the decision on that submission. The cell then refuses a `decide` that would record a second one. Nothing is appended.
- **A replacing decision does not count against itself.** When the deciding article has an output of type `decision`, its reductions read the chronicle without the decision the submission refers to.
- **The reduction's answer reaches the call.** Before the cell gives the deciding article the decision value of the contested decision, it answers that value again, on the day of the decision. Each field or finding that a reduction of the cell answers for the article of that decision gets the answer of that day (here `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar`). These reductions read the chronicle without the contested decision, because it is the decision under review. A date that a stage of that article requires of the cell is the day of the decision too, as in the new decision the cell records. The chronolexogram keeps the contested decision by its id, not those answers.

A row for a reduction carries the decision's fields, its outputs and its `id`, and nothing the cell adds. The school's reduction has a second input, `source: {decisions: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#7:11}`. It collects the ids of the decisions that a decision on objection revoked and leaves those out (`NOT` with `IN`). A `FOREACH` body does not see the binding of an outer `FOREACH`, so the link is a list of ids, not a nested loop.

| Case | Art. 13a anew | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Second leave in a school year | Refused again | Ongegrond, the decision stands |
| Profession judged anew on the employer's statement | Granted | Gegrond, revoked, leave granted |
| Refused anew on another ground | Refused again | Ongegrond: the outcomes do not name the ground |
| Late objection | Not run | Not admissible, the decision stands |
| Objection against the decision on an objection | Not run | Not admissible (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a) |
| Objection against the new decision taken on objection | Not run | Not admissible: its `taken_on` is 7:11 |
| Second objection against a decision already replaced | Would replace it again | The cell refuses the decision |

Not modeled: 6:6 and 6:11, 6:9 lid 2, the hearing (7:2), 7:10 lid 2 to 4, 7:12 lid 2 to 4.

### What moved out of the cell

The first version of this RFC put the procedure of Awb 7:11 in the cell (`packages/cel/src/objection.rs`). That has moved into rulework, except for one row in the table below that is still open.

| | First version | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Recording | `record_objection` next to `record_submission` | One `record_submission`, with `refers_to`. |
| Deciding | `decide_on_objection` next to `decide` | One `decide`. It runs the deciding article and records what that article produces. |
| Admissibility first | Order in Rust | 7:11 itself: the heroverweging is only read when `bezwaar_ontvankelijk` holds. |
| Heroverweging | The cell reruns the contested article | 7:11 calls the contested decision anew, with its own fields and findings and what is found anew. |
| "Aanleiding" | The cell compares outputs | 7:11 compares the outputs of the call with those of the contested decision. |
| Revoke and replace | The cell appends two chronolexograms | 7:11 gives the new decision as an output; the cell records both. |
| Awb names in the cell | `bestreden_besluit_herroepen` | None. The reduction reads the revocation by that name, in YAML. |
| Reduction in the nested call | The cell ran art. 13a anew with its reductions bound | The cell refreshes the fields of the decision value it passes. Open: a reduction that fills an open term of art. 13a with `implements`, so the engine finds it during the call. |
| A decision taken on objection | Refused by the cell | 7:11 reads `taken_on` of the contested decision, so it recognizes both the decision on an objection and the new decision it took. |
| Objection against a revoked decision | Refused by the cell | It is recorded and reconsidered. When the heroverweging would replace the decision again, the cell refuses the decision (a general rule, no Awb name). |
| A finding anew on an objection that is not admissible | Not possible | There is no heroverweging, so no article asks the finding, and the engine refuses it. |

The deciding authority may give a finding for an article that the decision calls anew. That finding is recorded among the fields of the decision on the objection. When that decision is called anew in turn, it does not pass the finding on: the article it calls anew takes the finding of the contested decision again. Beroep is not modeled, so nothing calls a decision on an objection anew yet.

The call has two more limits. A decision value holds a decision among its fields as an id. A call that merges those fields so passes an id where the article asks for a decision. The call also passes only the parameters the article itself declares and its findings per case. A value that only a hook on the decision or a stage of its procedure asks for does not reach it.

## Why

### Benefits

- "Gegrond" is no choice of the cell. It is art. 13a on the facts of that day, called by 7:11.
- The cell knows no Awb. Another article that decides on a submission referring to a decision works the same way.
- The reduction gives the whole answer. Whether a revoked decision counts is its rule.

### Tradeoffs

- "Differs in an outcome" is our reading of "aanleiding geeft". A heroverweging that refuses on another ground gives no cause: the outputs of art. 13a do not name the ground.
- Which article 7:11 calls is known only when it runs. The dependency graph and the editor do not see that edge, and the type checker knows the result only as a decision value.
- What is found anew is not in 7:11: it comes in from the top, apart from the parameters. A form for the deciding authority builds on `$bestreden_besluit.findings`.
- The new decision gets a period under Awb 6:7 and 6:8, recorded as `bezwaartermijn_einddatum`. For a decision taken on objection that is the period for appeal (Awb 7:1 lid 1 onder a); the name does not say so.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Awb 6:4 establishes the objection, 7:11 links to it** (`decides_on`, PR #1689). That adds a key; `requests` already does it.
- **The cell leaves revoked decisions out.** Then the reduction would not give the whole answer.
- **The cell runs 7:11** (the first version of this RFC). It needed `record_objection`, `decide_on_objection` and one Awb name in the cell.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.3: `BEZWAAR` in `produces.submission.kind`, as a list item's `type` and in `applies_to.submission`.
- Schema v0.8.4: `source.article`, `source.parameters_from`, the field type `decision` and `derived` on the decision item of `produces`. The engine calls the article (`resolve_article_call` in `packages/engine/src/service.rs`), checks a fixed target at load (T5 in `typecheck.rs`), calls a decision value anew and takes findings apart from the parameters (`evaluate_law_with_findings`), and compares lists and records strictly in `EQUALS`: an integer against a decimal of the same value is an error.
- Corpus: Awb 6:4, 6:5, 6:9, 7:2, 7:10 to 7:12 with `bezwaar.feature`, which passes the contested decision as a JSON object and what is found anew as findings; the school's reduction reads the decisions under 7:11.
- `packages/cel`: `record_submission` with `refers_to`, `decide` with findings and decision outputs, rows with `id`. The chronicle stores a decimal without loss ([#1732](https://github.com/NederlandseDigitaleDienst/regelrecht/pull/1732)), so a decision value read back and called anew compares equal to its stored outputs.
- Not built: beroep, the hearing, third parties, partial revocation, and replacing a decision a second time (the cell refuses it).
