## Context

The law format could not say two things.

- **An article requires that a fact exists.** "Een gehandicaptenparkeerkaart wordt niet afgegeven alvorens een geneeskundig onderzoek heeft plaatsgehad" (Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart art. 2 lid 1). The examination is not a decision and nobody applies for it, so it fits no `produces` item ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)).
- **Someone other than the decider is competent.** An earlier card may have come from another municipality or the CBR (art. 2 lid 2; BABW art. 49). The article's `competent_authority` names one authority, the one that decides ([RFC-002](/rfcs/rfc-002)).

## Decision

### 1. A fact an article requires

A `requires` entry may name a fact and the parameters it holds. The law says what the fact holds, not when it arises or who records it. The fact sits on the article that defines it (here art. 3 lid 1, who examines).

```yaml
# Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart, art. 3
machine_readable:
  requires:
    - fact: geneeskundig_onderzoek_verricht
      parameters: [bsn_aanvrager, datum_onderzoek, verricht_door]
      competent_authority:
        - name: Gemeentelijke Gezondheidsdienst
          type: CATEGORY
        - name: vanwege het gemeentelijk gezag aangewezen deskundige
          type: CATEGORY
```

The loader refuses a fact without parameters, a parameter the article lacks, a fact mixed with another provision, and two facts of one name.

### 2. Competence on an open term and on a fact

Both may carry their own `competent_authority` in the shape of RFC-002: one named authority or a list. The parking-card term is the example in [Competent Authority](/concepts/competent-authority#who-answers-an-open-term).

- On a term, it says who may answer it; on a fact, who establishes it.
- On the article: still who decides, one authority. A `#` reference stays allowed there (Wlz art. 2.1.1).
- The loader refuses a list on an article, an empty list, a `#` reference on a term or fact, and competence on a `requires` entry without `fact`.

### 3. One value from several answers

`combine` on a term merges the answers of several competent actors: `OR`, `AND`, `ADD`, `MIN`, `MAX`, fitting the term's type. The loader refuses it on a term without competent authorities, where only one actor is asked, and as `ONLY`, since two answers without a rule already fail.

### 4. Reading what another actor establishes

A law reads such a value as an output of the establishing article, with `source.regulation` ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)). The law names a role, never a cell, chronicle or register. Where a cell answers the term, the policy of the authority that decides binds the answer, and the engine asks the cells the term names wherever that binding runs ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048) §2 and §4). [RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046) says how a cell records.

### Where each choice comes from

| Choice | Source |
|---|---|
| A fact is a `requires` entry, not a `produces` item | Author's decision (Tim de Jager, 20 October 2026): a fact is not a besluit, and the law format holds no chronolexography words |
| The law gives a fact its shape, not a cell file | Author's decision (Tim de Jager, 20 October 2026); departs from RFC-022 §1.3 |
| Competence on the term or the fact | Author's decision (Tim de Jager, 20 October 2026), after a list on the article mixed deciding with answering |
| On a fact, competence names who establishes it | Author's decision (Tim de Jager); recorder and competent authority stay apart (RFC-022 §1.3) and coincide only when a cell establishes the fact itself |
| A reading term repeats the fact's competence | Own choice: deriving it would need the engine to link term and fact |
| `combine` on the term, with `FOREACH` values | Author's decision (Tim de Jager) |

## Why

### Benefits

- Fact and competence are said in words of law. A jurist needs no idea of cells.
- The law names a role. A municipality, the CBR or a GGD answers because the law makes it competent.

### Tradeoffs

- A fact and the term that reads it both name the same authorities.

Open points are listed in [Open Points in Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **An `EXECUTOGRAM` item in `produces`.** Built, dropped: a fact is not a besluit, and it put chronolexography in the law format.
- **Fact streams declared with the cell** (`executogrammen/`, schema v0.8.6) or in `chronicles/` (RFC-022 §1.3). Built, dropped: the cell, not the law, would give the shape.
- **`answered_from` on the reading term.** A new word for what `source.regulation` says.
- **A cell id in `source.regulation`** (RFC-022 §4.2). The law would name a cell, not a role.
- **A list of authorities on the article.** Mixes deciding with answering.

## Implementation

Schema v0.8.7: `fact`, `parameters`, `description` on `requires`. Schema v0.8.8: `competent_authority` on a term and a fact, `combine` on a term. Law model: `ArticleRequirement::fact`, `ArticleRequirement::competent_authority`, `OpenTerm::competent_authority`, `OpenTerm::combine`, `CompetentAuthority::List`; checks in `load_check::reject_malformed_facts` and `load_check::reject_misplaced_competence`. Corpus: Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart art. 2 and 3; `fictief_beleid_vestiging_obs_de_klimop` art. 1 requires the fact `vestiging`, since no article in the corpus does.
