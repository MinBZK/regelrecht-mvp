## Context

Two things the law says could not be written down.

**An article requires that a fact exists.** Art. 2 lid 1 of the Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart: "Een gehandicaptenparkeerkaart wordt niet afgegeven alvorens een geneeskundig onderzoek heeft plaatsgehad met betrekking tot de handicap van de aanvrager." Art. 3 lid 1 says who examines: the GGD, or "een vanwege het gemeentelijk gezag aangewezen deskundige". The examination is no decision and nobody applies for it, so it fits no `produces` item ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)). Where a school is established, which fixes its region for the school holidays (Regeling vaststelling schoolvakanties 2025–2030, art. 2), is a fact of the same kind.

**Someone other than the decider is competent.** Regeling art. 2 lid 2 lets the examination be left out when "aan de aanvrager eerder een gehandicaptenparkeerkaart is verstrekt". That card may come from another municipality or from the CBR (BABW art. 49 lid 1 and 3). `competent_authority` on the article names one authority, the one that decides ([RFC-002](/rfcs/rfc-002)). It cannot say who may answer an open term, or who establishes a fact.

## Decision

### 1. A fact an article requires

A `requires` entry may name a fact with the parameters it holds. The law says what the fact holds; it does not say when the fact arises or who records it.

```yaml
# Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart, art. 3
machine_readable:
  requires:
    - fact: geneeskundig_onderzoek_verricht
      parameters: [bsn_aanvrager, datum_onderzoek, verricht_door]
      competent_authority:
        - name: Gemeentelijke Gezondheidsdienst
          type: CATEGORY
        - name: vanwege het gemeentelijk gezag aangewezen deskundige
          type: CATEGORY
```

The fact sits on the article that defines it (art. 3 lid 1, who examines), not on the article that requires it to have taken place. The parameters are those of the article, with their types. The loader refuses a fact without parameters, a parameter the article does not declare, a fact and another provision in one entry, and two facts of one name on an article.

### 2. Competence on an open term and on a fact

An open term and a fact may each carry their own `competent_authority`, in the shape of RFC-002: one named authority or a list. On a term it says who may answer it, on a fact who establishes it. On the article the field keeps its meaning: who decides, one authority, where a `#` reference to a computed output stays allowed (Wlz art. 2.1.1). The loader refuses a list on an article, an empty list, a `#` reference on a term or a fact, and a `competent_authority` on a `requires` entry without `fact`.

```yaml
# Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart, art. 2
open_terms:
  - id: aanvrager_eerder_gehandicaptenparkeerkaart_verstrekt
    type: boolean
    required: true
    decided_per_case_by: >-
      de verstrekkende instantie: het college of het CBR (artikel 49
      van het BABW)
    competent_authority:
      - name: college van burgemeester en wethouders
        type: CATEGORY
      - name: CBR
    combine: OR
```

### 3. One value from several answers

`combine` on an open term makes one value of the answers of several competent actors, with the values of `FOREACH`: `OR`, `AND`, `ADD`, `MIN`, `MAX`. It must fit the term's type. The loader refuses `combine` on a term that names no competent authorities, since only one actor is asked for it, and refuses `ONLY`, because two answers without a rule already fail.

### 4. Reading what another actor establishes

A law that needs such a value reads the output of the establishing article with `source.regulation` ([RFC-007](/rfcs/rfc-007)). That article declares the fact as an open term and gives it as an output. The law names a role, never a cell, a chronicle or a register.

How the engine asks the competent actors, and how a cell answers, is [RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048). How a cell records a fact is [RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046).

### Where each choice comes from

| Choice | Source |
|---|---|
| A fact is a `requires` entry, not a `produces` item | Owner's decision (20 October 2026): a besluit is something the law produces, a fact is not, and the law format holds no word of chronolexography |
| The shape of a fact follows from the law, not from a file of the cell | Owner's decision (20 October 2026); this departs from RFC-022 §1.3, which declares an executogram in a chronicle-stream file |
| Competence on the term or the fact, in the shape of RFC-002 | Owner's decision (20 October 2026), after a first version put a list on the article and mixed who decides with who may answer |
| On a fact, `competent_authority` names who establishes it | Owner's decision; RFC-022 §1.3 keeps the recording actor and the competent authority apart, and that still holds: where a cell establishes the fact itself the two coincide |
| A term that reads a fact names its own competence, equal to the fact's | Own choice: deriving it from the fact would need the engine to link a term to a fact |
| `combine` on the term, with the values of `FOREACH` | Owner's decision |

## Why

### Benefits

- The law says what a fact holds and who is competent for it, in words of law. A jurist reads both without knowing what a cell is.
- The law names a role. A municipality, the CBR and a GGD answer because the law makes them competent, not because a rulework names their system.

### Tradeoffs

- A fact and the term that reads it each name the same authorities, once in each place.

Open questions are on [Open Points](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **An `EXECUTOGRAM` item of `produces`.** Built, then dropped: a fact is no besluit the law produces, and the item put a word of chronolexography in the law format.
- **Fact streams declared with the cell** (`corpus/cells/<cell>/executogrammen/`, schema v0.8.6), or in a top-level `chronicles/` as RFC-022 §1.3 has it. Built, then dropped: the shape then follows from the cell, not from the law.
- **A field `answered_from` on the reading term**, pointing at the establishing article. A new word, where `source.regulation` already says it.
- **A cell id in `source.regulation`**, as RFC-022 §4.2 sketches. The law would name a cell instead of a role.
- **A list of authorities on the article.** It mixes who decides with who may answer, and an article can establish several terms with different actors.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.7: `fact`, `parameters` and `description` on a `requires` entry. Schema v0.8.8: `competent_authority` on an open term and on a fact, `combine` on an open term.
- Law model: `ArticleRequirement::fact`, `ArticleRequirement::competent_authority`, `OpenTerm::competent_authority`, `OpenTerm::combine`, `CompetentAuthority::List`; the loader checks in `load_check::reject_malformed_facts` and `load_check::reject_misplaced_competence`.
- Corpus: Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart art. 2 and 3; the fictitious policy `fictief_beleid_vestiging_obs_de_klimop`, art. 1, which requires the fact `vestiging` because no article in the corpus does.
