## Context

Some answers are in what an organization recorded earlier, not in the law:

- The head grants leave "slechts eenmaal ... per schooljaar" (Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 2). Earlier grants are in the school's own decisions.
- Leave must fall "buiten de schoolvakanties" (art. 11 onder f). The holidays depend on where the school is established, a fact the school recorded.
- An examination may be skipped if "aan de aanvrager eerder een gehandicaptenparkeerkaart is verstrekt" (Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart art. 2 lid 2). Another municipality or the CBR may have issued it.

The law leaves such terms to the authority and does not say how the authority reads its records back. The [position paper](https://chronolexografie.nl/position-paper/) does: "Reductie vindt altijd plaats ín de cel waar de betreffende chronolexogrammen zijn vastgelegd", and "Synthese gebeurt bij de afnemer, niet bij de bron". The source is the cell. The consumer is the law together with the policy of the authority that decides: the law asks, the policy says where the answer comes from.

## Decision

[Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography#answering-from-the-chronicle) shows this at work.

### 1. A reduction is a named answer of its cell

How a cell reduces is up to the cell. The engine only asks it for an answer by name (§2). The cell in `packages/cel` uses what the engine offers: a reduction in the law format, `regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE`. It sits in the corpus with the rest of the rulework, at `corpus/regulation/nl/reducties/<id>/<valid_from>.yaml`, so it is type-checked and traced like any article. The loader also applies the checks in §3. Its `competent_authority` names its holder, the body whose chronicle it reads. Each output is an answer of the holder's cell; a reduction without a holder is called by other reductions and gives no answer (below). A reduction fills no open term, so it has no `implements`. Which question an answer serves is for the one who asks, so one answer can serve terms in several laws (§2).

```yaml
# reductie_verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar, shortened
competent_authority:
  name: Het hoofd van OBS De Klimop
execution:
  parameters: [bsn_jongere, eerste_verlofdag]
  input:
    - name: besluiten
      source:
        decisions: leerplichtwet_1969#13a
    - name: begin_schooljaar
      source:
        regulation: fictief_beleid_vakantieverlof_obs_de_klimop
        output: begin_schooljaar_van_verlof
        parameters: {eerste_verlofdag: $eerste_verlofdag}
  output:
    - name: verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar
```

- `source.decisions` (`<regulation>#<article>`): one row per decision, with fields, outputs and `id`.
- `source.facts` (`<regulation>#<article>#<fact>`): one row per recorded fact ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). The last `#` separates the fact.
- `combine: ONLY` on a `FOREACH`: the one result the filter leaves; null for none, an error for two.
- A reduction may call policy or law with `source.regulation` for a norm, and passes what it found in the chronicle as parameters, so a norm is written once, in policy. Above, the head's policy says which school year the leave falls in; the reduction applies it to the rows.
- A reduction may call another reduction the same way. One without `competent_authority` has no holder, so no cell gives it as an answer. It takes its rows as parameters from the reduction that calls it. A norm that every administrative body applies to its own decisions is then written once. The reduction of the Awb says which decisions still stand after objection and appeal (Awb 7:11, 8:72), and which decisions the court set in their place (8:72 lid 3 onder b).

The cell's `cel.yaml` is its catalogue: per answer the reduction that gives it. It holds no logic.

```yaml
# corpus/cells/obs_de_klimop/cel.yaml
name: Het hoofd van OBS De Klimop
category: het hoofd
answers:
  verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar: reductie_verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar
```

### 2. The decider's policy binds a term to an answer

The law asks no cell. Which term an answer fills is the consumer's choice, made in the `UITVOERINGSBELEID` of the authority that decides. One article fills the term with `implements` and takes the answer as an input:

```yaml
# fictief_beleid_vakantieverlof_obs_de_klimop art. 2, shortened
implements:
  - {law: leerplichtwet_1969, article: 13a, open_term: verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar}
execution:
  input:
    - name: antwoord_verlof_al_verleend
      nullable: true
      source:
        reduction: verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar
        parameters: {bsn_jongere: $bsn_jongere, eerste_verlofdag: $eerste_verlofdag}
```

A caller may give an execution an answerer (`OpenTermAnswerer`, in a `Call`). Wherever the binding runs, at any depth, the engine asks it for the answer by name with the parameters the binding passes. During a decision the cell is the answerer. It runs the reduction its catalogue names and returns the value with its provenance.

- The binding is policy like any other, so the order of precedence is unchanged: policy, then the finding, then the default ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) §5). Where the cell answers, its answer sets a finding aside.
- Without an answer the input is null (hence `nullable: true`). On a term with a default or `decided_per_case_by`, the binding is then silent and the term falls back; a term decided per case without a finding fails the execution, so a missing answer never grants anything. On a term with neither, null is its value.
- The binding passes only the parameters it names. The cell refuses one the reduction does not declare, and a declared one the binding leaves out unless it is `required: false`.
- Should the term occur in another article, or in another law the same authority carries out, the policy gets a second article with the same source. The reduction stays as it is.
- The engine never runs a reduction itself; a `REDUCTIE` stays out of its implements index.

### 3. Only a reduction reads a chronicle

The loader (`load_check::reject_misplaced_reduction`) keeps the chronicle out of law and policy:

- `source.decisions` and `source.facts` only in a `REDUCTIE`, alone in their `source`;
- in a `REDUCTIE` no `implements`, `hooks`, `overrides`, `execution.produces` or `open_terms`;
- `source.reduction` only in an article with `implements`, in a `UITVOERINGSBELEID` that names one holder by name, from schema v0.8.9, and beside no other key of `source` except `parameters` and `combine`;
- `combine` in a `source` only beside `reduction`, fitting the input's type.

Schema v0.8.5 to v0.8.8 allowed `implements` on a `REDUCTIE`. The loader still accepts it under a `$schema` of those versions and gives it no meaning; a reduction without `$schema` is held to the current version.

The cell refuses an answer from a reduction the service does not hold, from one that is no `REDUCTIE`, and from one whose `competent_authority` is not the cell. A cell directory with `reducties/` does not open, since a reduction sits in the corpus. Before the first `valid_from` of a reduction the cell is silent, so a decision from before it replays with the authority's finding. `Cell::answers` lists the catalogue. Declaring it is a RegelRecht addition (RFC-022 §4.1); in the paper a cell reduces on request and declares nothing.

### 4. The process combines the answers of competent cells

A decision may take other cells along: its sphere. Which cells the engine asks for a binding's answer follows from the term it fills:

- the term names `competent_authority`: every answerer of the call it names, an `INSTANCE` by name and a `CATEGORY` by category, in name order and without short-circuit;
- otherwise: the cell of the policy's holder.

This holds wherever the binding runs. [RFC-052](/rfcs/rfc-052) routed on competence only for a term reached through `source.regulation`; with the answer named in policy that limit had no work left to do. Whether to keep the narrower rule is an [open point](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

Every cell is asked for the same answer name. A cell whose catalogue lacks it is silent, as is one that answers null.

| Answers | Value |
|---|---|
| none | null, as in §2 |
| one | that answer |
| several | merged with `combine`: the term's ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051) §3), else the binding's; without either, an error naming the answerers |

The law's `combine` wins, since it says how the law weighs several competent actors. The binding's serves a term that names none.

### 5. Who answered is in the result, why is with the cell

`ArticleResult.open_terms` reports `filled_by: {answer: {law_id, article, answers}}`: the binding's policy and article, and per answer the cell, the answer's name, the reduction with its version, and how far it read the chronicle. The trace has a node per answer under the binding's input, null answers included; a cell without the answer is named in the input's message. Why is the cell's to show (`Cell::explain_answer`), with the steps of the policy the reduction calls; that trace is not stored.

### 6. Replay and reconsideration

On replay ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)) the cell answers from its chronicle as it stood before the decision. Sphere cells answer from what they recorded up to its day. Only that day is stored, since each sphere cell can answer again from its own chronicle. What a late record does to a replay is an [open point](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points). When Awb 7:11 calls a decision anew, the binding runs in that call and the cell answers without the contested decision. Revoked and annulled decisions stay in the rows; the reduction of the Awb leaves them out.

## Why

### Benefits

- Law and policy say nothing about the chronicle. A jurist reads both without knowing what a cell is; the policy names an answer in words.
- The source is not tied to one question. The consumer binds the answer where it needs it.
- A norm such as the school year is written once, in policy, and the reduction applies it.
- Anyone can answer, however it reduces: a cell, or a scenario with the authority's finding.
- A reduction is type-checked and traced like any article.
- The engine knows no cells; it asks for an answer by name and compares names.

### Tradeoffs

- An authority's finding on a term the cell answers is set aside; the trace says so.
- A decision's trace shows who answered, not why. For why, ask the cell.
- The catalogue is the only record of which answers are the cell's. A cell opened from the wrong directory, whose catalogue lacks the answer, replays it as the authority's finding with the same outcome.

Open points are listed in [Open Points in Chronolexography](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **The reduction returns a list; policy computes.** Policy would hold logic that exists only for the chronicle.
- **A reduction or binding language in cell configuration** (PR #1689, RFC-022 §4.1). A second language without type checker or trace.
- **The article asks the answer as a parameter** (first version). Lost in nested calls, invisible in the trace.
- **The engine runs the reduction as an implementation** (16–17 October 2026). That put the reduction's `FOREACH` in the decision's trace.
- **The reduction fills the term with `implements`** (schema v0.8.5 to v0.8.8). It works, but ties the source to one question of one law.
- **Policy reads the chronicle itself.** That removes the line between consumer and source.
- **The cell drops revoked decisions from the rows.** The reduction would not see the whole picture.
- **A list of answers as a value, with `FOREACH` in the law.** The law would see cells.
- **A composite answerer in `packages/cel`.** Rule and competence in Rust; the engine would see one source.

Engine: `answer.rs` (`OpenTermAnswerer::answer`, `AnswerRequest`, `Answer`, `AnswerProvenance`, `Call`, `Authority`), `resolve_open_term`, `resolve_answer`, `combine_with`, `combine_only`, `FilledBy::Answer`, `load_check::chronicle_read`, `load_check::reject_misplaced_reduction`; tests in `packages/engine/tests/reduction.rs` and `synthesis.rs`. Not built: calling an article per `FOREACH` element, so a reduction asks policy once for the case and compares each row with that.
