## Context

Some answers the law asks for are in what an organization recorded earlier, not in the law.

- The head grants leave "slechts eenmaal ... per schooljaar" (Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a lid 2). Whether leave was granted already is in the school's own decisions.
- Leave must fall "buiten de schoolvakanties" (art. 11 onder f). The holidays follow from a regulation and from where the school is established, which the school recorded as a fact.
- A medical examination may be left out when "aan de aanvrager eerder een gehandicaptenparkeerkaart is verstrekt" (Regeling gehandicaptenparkeerkaart art. 2 lid 2). That card may have come from another municipality or from the CBR, so the municipality that decides does not hold the answer alone.

Art. 13a leaves `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar` to the head per case and does not say how the head reads its own decisions back. The [position paper](https://chronolexografie.nl/position-paper/) does: "Reductie vindt altijd plaats ín de cel waar de betreffende chronolexogrammen zijn vastgelegd", and "Synthese gebeurt bij de afnemer, niet bij de bron". The law defines the interface of a cell; the cell decides how it answers.

## Decision

### 1. A reduction is rulework of its own kind

A reduction (`regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE`) lives with its cell, at `reducties/<id>/<valid_from>.yaml`. It reads the cell's chronicle and fills one open term (`implements`):

```yaml
# corpus/cells/obs_de_klimop/reducties/reductie_verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar
implements:
  - {law: leerplichtwet_1969, article: 13a, open_term: verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar}
input:
  - name: besluiten
    type: array
    source:
      decisions: leerplichtwet_1969#13a
  - name: begin_schooljaar
    type: date
    source:
      regulation: fictief_beleid_schooljaargrenzen_obs_de_klimop
      parameters: {datum: $eerste_verlofdag}
```

- `source: {decisions: <regulation>#<article>}` gives one row per decision taken under that article, with its fields, outputs and `id`.
- `source: {facts: <regulation>#<article>#<fact>}` gives one row per recorded instance of a fact ([RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051)). An article number holds no `#`, so the last one separates the fact.
- `combine: ONLY` on a `FOREACH` gives the one result its filter leaves: null for none, an error for two. A reduction picks the record in force on a day with it.
- Only a `REDUCTIE` may read the chronicle. It has no `hooks`, `overrides`, `execution.produces` or `open_terms`, and it may call other rulework as usual.

### 2. The engine asks, the cell answers

A caller may give an execution an answerer (`OpenTermAnswerer`, in a `Call` next to the findings). When an article reads an open term, at any depth, the engine asks the answerer first. During a decision the cell is that answerer. It answers only a term that one of its reductions in force names in `implements`: it runs the reduction on its own chronicle, with the parameters the reduction declares taken by name from the article that asks, or from the nearest question around it.

The answer comes before the order of [RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045) §5: it wins over an implementation and over a finding. On a term with a default or with `decided_per_case_by`, a null answer is silence and the term falls back on the finding or the default; on any other term null is the value. Without any of them the execution fails, so a missing answer never grants a second leave. The engine never runs a reduction by itself; a `REDUCTIE` stays out of its implements index. Outside a cell nothing answers, and a scenario gives the authority's finding.

### 3. The directory says which terms are the cell's

A term that a reduction in the cell's `reducties/` implements, in any version, is the cell's. On a day on which no reduction in force fills it, the cell refuses the decision. Before that day the term was the authority's, so a decision taken then still replays. Opening the cell refuses a reduction that fills no term, a version whose `$id` or file name does not match, and any other entry under `reducties/`. Before each decision the cell also refuses a reduction in force that fills a term its article does not declare that day, or reads a fact its article does not require.

`Cell::answers` lists per version of each reduction the term it fills. That a cell declares what it can answer is a RegelRecht addition; it comes from RFC-022 §4.1, not from the paper.

### 4. The process combines the answers of competent cells

A decision may take other cells along, its sphere. For an open term that names a `competent_authority`, of an article that a `source.regulation` read reaches, the engine asks every answerer of the call the term names: the deciding cell and the cells of the sphere. An `INSTANCE` matches the answerer's name, a `CATEGORY` its category. All of them are asked, in the order of their names and without short-circuit; no `combine` value depends on that order. When none is competent, the trace says so and the term falls back. A sphere in a proof of concept is never complete.

| Answers | Value |
|---|---|
| none, or only nulls | the term falls back on its finding or its default |
| one | that answer |
| several | combined with the term's `combine`; without `combine` an error that names the answerers |

The loader's checks on `combine` are in [RFC-051](/rfcs/rfc-051) §3. Everywhere else only the deciding cell is asked: at the top of an execution, in a call through `source.article`, and for a term without `competent_authority`. A reduction asks only its own cell. Before each decision every cell, the deciding one and those of the sphere, refuses a reduction that fills a term whose `competent_authority` does not name it.

### 5. Who answered is in the result, why is with the cell

`ArticleResult.open_terms` reports an answered term as `filled_by: {answer: ...}`, or `answers` for several combined, with the answerer, the reduction and the version that answered, and how far it read the chronicle. The trace has one node for the term. For a term routed to several cells that node holds a node per answerer, silent ones included.

The reasoning stays with the cell. `Cell::explain_answer` replays the decision and runs the reduction for a term with tracing: which rows it read and which its filter skipped. That trace is not stored.

### 6. Replay and reconsideration

A replay ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)) has the cell answer from its chronicle as it stood before the decision, and the cells of the sphere from what they recorded up to its day; the decision stores only that day. When Awb 7:11 calls a decision anew, the engine asks the cell in that call, and the cell reads its chronicle without the contested decision. The school's reduction reads the decisions under 7:11 as a second input and leaves out a grant that one of them revoked.

### Where each choice comes from

| Choice | Source |
|---|---|
| Reduction happens in the cell that holds the chronolexograms | The paper |
| Synthesis happens in the process, never in a cell | The paper: "Synthese gebeurt bij de afnemer, niet bij de bron" |
| Routing on competence only for an article reached through `source.regulation` | Own choice: the deciding cell's own judgements and a decision called anew stay with it |
| One answer stays `FilledBy::Answer` | Own choice: a consumer that reads one answerer keeps working |
| A replay asks other cells as of the decision's day, and stores only that day | Owner's decision |

## Why

### Benefits

- Law and policy contain nothing about the chronicle. The school-year rule is written once, in policy, and the reduction asks it.
- Anyone can supply the answer: a cell, or a BDD scenario that passes the authority's finding.
- A reduction uses the rule language of an article, so it is type-checked and traced the same way.
- The engine knows no cells. It asks an answerer and compares two names.
- A value one actor establishes is answered once. A Chamber of Commerce cell would answer the type of registration on the establishing article of the Handelsregisterwet, and every law that needs it reads that output: one `implements`, not one per asking law.

### Tradeoffs

- The authority can still give a finding on a term the cell answers, and it is set aside; the trace says so.
- The trace of a decision shows who answered, not why. A reader who wants why asks the cell.

Limitations and open questions are on [Open Points](/concepts/chronolexography-open-points).

### Alternatives Considered

- **The reduction returns a list, and policy computes from it.** Policy would hold logic that exists only because of the chronicle.
- **A binding or a reduction language in cell configuration** (PR #1689, the sketch in RFC-022 §4.1). Cell configuration is a last resort, and a second language would have no type checker or trace.
- **The article asks the answer as a parameter** (the first version). In a nested call the engine could not find it, and the trace did not show where it came from.
- **The engine runs the reduction as an implementation** (16 to 17 October 2026). The decision's trace then held the reduction's `FOREACH`, and the cell had to bind its chronicle as a data source.
- **The cell leaves revoked decisions out of the rows.** Then the reduction would not give the whole answer.
- **A list of answers as a value, with a `FOREACH` in the law.** The law would see cells, and an open term cannot be a list.
- **A composite answerer in `packages/cel`.** The rule and the competence would sit in Rust, and the engine would see one source.

| Schema | Adds |
|---|---|

- Engine: `answer.rs` (`OpenTermAnswerer`, `Call` with its sphere, `Authority`), asked in `resolve_open_term` and routed in `ask_answerers`; `combine_with` and `combine_only` in `operations.rs`; `FilledBy::Answer` and `FilledBy::Answers`; `load_check::chronicle_read` and `load_check::reject_misplaced_reduction`. - Corpus: the reductions under `corpus/cells/`, for OBS De Klimop, the municipalities Wilgendaal and Oostmeer, the CBR and the GGD.
