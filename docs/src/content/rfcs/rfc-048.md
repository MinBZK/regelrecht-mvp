## Decision

The reduction (RFC-022 §4.1, *chronolexoreductie*) takes place in the cell, over its own chronicle. Its rule is an article of the recording actor's policy, in the law format, run by the engine. The lexostatus is that article's output.

1. **The law says what is needed.** Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a gets the open term `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar` and refuses leave when it holds (lid 2: "slechts eenmaal ... per schooljaar").
2. **The policy says how it follows from earlier decisions.** `fictief_beleid_verleend_verlof_obs_de_klimop` fills the term with `implements`. Its one input `eerdere_besluiten` is an `array` with `source: {}`, read with `FOREACH`.
3. **The cell recognizes the list by its shape.** An `array` input with an empty source, in a policy that fills an open term of the deciding article, is the chronicle. No configuration and no binding name.
4. **The cell answers with its earlier decisions under that article.** One row per decision, holding its fields and outputs by their own names. Which ones count is the policy's to say.
5. **The decision records the value.** It lands in `fields`. That it came from policy, and which article read what, follows from the law in force on `recorded_on`, so the chronolexogram repeats none of it.

This replaces the sketch of RFC-022 §4.1, which put the rule of a reduction in cell configuration.

### 1. What counts as "already granted"

The fictitious policy of the school decides, marked FICTIEF:

- A decision counts when it granted leave (`verlof_verleend: true`) to the same young person.
- Its first leave day falls in the same school year as the first day of the leave asked. Leave across two school years counts for the first.
- A school year runs from 1 August to 31 July. The Leerplichtwet does not define it. The Wet op het primair onderwijs (art. 1) uses the same period but is not in the corpus, so the policy has no `legal_basis`.
- A refusal does not count.

### 2. What the cell does

`decision::derive` collects the list inputs of the filling policies as `ChronicleInput`. `Cell::decide` registers one data source per list, scoped to the policy, fills it with `reading::rows`, and removes it after the decision. It refuses when another data source already answers the list. That is why `decide` takes the service mutably.

A row of the chronicle is the decision's `fields` merged with its `outputs`. A name in both with different values is refused. Applications are left out: an application holds nothing the policy can count, and the decision on it holds what the application gave.

### 3. Without the cell

The policy runs without a cell. A BDD scenario passes the list as a parameter (`parameter "eerdere_besluiten" is the collection`) or as a data source keyed on `bsn_jongere`. Art. 13a then reads the same policy.

## Why

### Benefits

- One rule language. The reduction is type-checked, traced and tested like any article, and a jurist reads it next to art. 13a.
- The law stays free of chronolex words, and so does the policy: it reads "eerdere besluiten", not a chronicle.
- The cell holds no rules. Changing what counts is a policy change with an owner.

### Tradeoffs

- The cell answers every `array` input with an empty source in a filling policy. A policy that wants such a list from elsewhere cannot have it in a cell; the cell refuses rather than shadow another source.
- The school year of the leave follows from its first leave day, but the first lesdag comes from the policy in force on the day of the decision. Leave in the next school year, decided in the current one, is checked against the current first lesdag.
- The rows are the decisions under the deciding article only. A policy that needs decisions under another article waits for a next step.
- A FOREACH element hides a parameter of the same name (RFC-016), so the policy copies the asked values into helper outputs first.

### Alternatives Considered

**A `registers:` binding in cell configuration** (PR #1689). It names which policy reads which chronicle. Here the shape already says it, and the owner keeps cell configuration as a last resort.

**A reduction language in cell configuration** (RFC-022 §4.1 sketch, the chronolex proof of concept). It repeats FOREACH without a type checker, trace or conformance suite.

**A parameter of art. 13a with the earlier decisions.** It would make them part of the application, and the parent does not supply them.

## Implementation

- Corpus: art. 13a with `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar`; `fictief_beleid_verleend_verlof_obs_de_klimop` with a scenario next to it; the school-year policy for 2027-2028.
- `packages/cel`: `ChronicleInput`, `reading`, `Cell::decide` binding the chronicle for one decision.
- No schema change. The engine gains `DataSourceRegistry::has_field_for_law`, so the cell checks only the sources that take part for the policy.
- Not built: which chronolexograms a value read (RFC-022 §4.1 result listing), lists of other articles, a register outside the cell.
