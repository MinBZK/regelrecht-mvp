## Decision

The reduction (RFC-022 §4.1, *chronolexoreductie*) is rulework of its own kind, `regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE`. It is kept with the cell, reads the decisions in its chronicle and yields a list. The policy reasons about that list and knows nothing of decisions.

1. **The law says what is needed.** Leerplichtwet 1969 art. 13a gets the open term `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar` and refuses leave when it holds (lid 2: "slechts eenmaal ... per schooljaar").
2. **The policy reasons about granted leave.** `fictief_beleid_verleend_verlof_obs_de_klimop` fills the term. Its input `verleende_verloven` is an `array` with `source: {}`: one row per granted leave, with `bsn_jongere` and `eerste_verlofdag`. Who supplies the list, the policy does not say.
3. **The reduction turns decisions into that list.** `reductie_verleende_verloven` reads `besluiten` with `source: {decisions: leerplichtwet_1969#13a}` and yields the ones with `verlof_verleend: true`. It is stored at `corpus/cells/obs_de_klimop/reducties/`, not in `corpus/regulation/`.
4. **The cell connects them by name.** For an input without a source of a policy that fills an open term of the deciding article, the cell looks for a reduction with an output by that name. It runs the reduction over its decisions and registers the output as a data source for that policy, for one decision.
5. **The decision records the value.** It lands in `fields`. Which article read what follows from the law in force on `recorded_on`, so the chronolexogram repeats none of it.

This replaces the sketch of RFC-022 §4.1, which put the rule of a reduction in cell configuration.

### 1. The contract: `source: {decisions}`

`source.decisions` names an article, `<regulation>#<article>`, and means "the decisions taken under this article". A jurist can read it without the word chronicle.

- A row is one decision: the fields and the outputs of the decision item of that article, by their own names. A name in both with different values is refused.
- Only a data source answers it, or the caller with a parameter of that name, as a test does. No regulation is executed, and no source is an error, never an empty list.
- It stands alone: no `regulation`, `output`, `parameters` or `endpoint` beside it.
- Only a `REDUCTIE` may read it, and a `REDUCTIE` does not act on law: no `implements`, `hooks` or `overrides`. The schema says both (`if`/`then`/`else` on the layer), and the loader refuses both too.

### 2. What counts as "already granted"

The two rulework kinds split the question.

- The reduction: a decision under art. 13a that granted leave. A refusal is no granted leave.
- The policy, marked FICTIEF: granted leave of the same young person whose first leave day falls in the school year of the leave asked. Leave across two school years counts for the first.
- A school year runs from 1 August to 31 July. The Leerplichtwet does not define it. The Wet op het primair onderwijs (art. 1) uses the same period but is not in the corpus, so the policy has no `legal_basis`.

### 3. What the cell does

`Cell::with_reductions` reads the reductions from the cell directory, one version per file at `reducties/<id>/<valid_from>.yaml`. It refuses rulework of another kind, and a reduction outside the directory of its `$id`. `Cell::decide` binds them through `reading::bind`, which:

- loads the reductions into the service and unloads them after the decision;
- answers each `decisions` input with the chronicle's rows, scoped to the reduction;
- runs a reduction only when a policy asks for one of its outputs, and registers that output scoped to the policy;
- refuses, before anything runs, a source that already answers one of those inputs, two reductions with one output, an output of another type than the input, and a law already loaded under a reduction's `$id`.

## Why

### Benefits

- The policy is free of chronolex knowledge. It reads granted leave, so a jurist checks it against art. 13a without knowing what a cell is.
- Any supplier can feed it: a BDD scenario passes `verleende_verloven` as a parameter, another system as a data source scoped to the policy.
- One rule language. The reduction is type-checked, traced and tested like an article.
- The reduction is not law. It lives outside `corpus/regulation`, so the corpus loaders do not load it, and its layer keeps it from filling an open term, hooking onto a decision or replacing an output.

### Tradeoffs

- Only the directory keeps a reduction out of the corpus. Nothing yet refuses a `REDUCTIE` placed under `corpus/regulation` or created in the editor.
- The link from reduction to policy is a name. A reduction output and a policy input that share a name by accident are joined; the type check and the refusal to shadow another source catch only part of that.
- The format has no operation that builds a row, so a granted leave is the whole row of the decision. The policy reads two fields of it.
- The school year of the leave follows from its first leave day, but the first lesdag comes from the policy in force on the day of the decision.
- A FOREACH element hides a parameter of the same name (RFC-016), so the policy copies the asked values into helper outputs first.

### Alternatives Considered

**The policy reads the decisions, and the cell recognizes its list by shape** (the first version of this RFC). An `array` input with an empty source was taken to be the chronicle. That leaked chronolex knowledge into the policy: it had to know what a decision row holds and that refusals are in it.

**A `registers:` binding in cell configuration** (PR #1689). It names which policy reads which chronicle. The owner keeps cell configuration as a last resort.

**A reduction language in cell configuration** (RFC-022 §4.1 sketch, the chronolex proof of concept). It repeats FOREACH without a type checker, trace or conformance suite.

**The reduction fills the policy's input with `implements`.** That makes the reduction a regulation that fills law, which it is not, and ties the policy to a supplier.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.2: `regulatory_layer: REDUCTIE` and `source.decisions`, with the layer rules.
- Engine: `Source::decisions`; a `decisions` input resolves through a data source only; `load_check::reject_misplaced_reduction`.
- Corpus: art. 13a with `verlof_al_verleend_in_schooljaar`; the policy with a scenario next to it; the reduction under `corpus/cells/obs_de_klimop/`, validated by `just validate` and loaded only by the cell.
- `packages/cel`: `Reduction`, `PolicyInput`, `reading::bind`, `Cell::with_reductions`.
- Not built: which chronolexograms a value read (RFC-022 §4.1 result listing), a reduction across cells.
