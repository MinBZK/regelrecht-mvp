## Context

After the objection ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)) the Awb gives one more step to whoever disagrees with a decision: "Een belanghebbende kan tegen een besluit beroep instellen bij de bestuursrechter" (Awb 8:1). The parent whose leave was refused, and whose objection was declared unfounded, can go to court. The court then judges whether the decision on the objection stands. Its judgment is not a decision of the school.

The appeal differs from the objection in two ways that matter for the model. Another body decides, and a court is not an administrative body (Awb 1:1 lid 2 onder c). The outcome differs as well. On an objection the administrative body revokes its decision and takes a new one itself (Awb 7:11 lid 2). The court annuls the decision (Awb 8:72 lid 1), and the legal effects go with it (lid 2) unless the court keeps them (lid 3 onder a). It can then order the administrative body to take a new decision within a term (lid 4).

This RFC covers the appeal in rulework. How the judgment travels from the court to the school is the subject of later sections, added as they are built.

## Decision

1. **Awb 8:70 establishes the appeal and decides on it**, as 7:11 does for the objection. `produces` lists a `BEROEP` that `requests` the decision of 8:70 and holds `bestreden_besluit`, and an `UITSPRAAK` with what the judgment holds.
2. **The judgment is not a besluit.** Its kind is `UITSPRAAK`, not `BESCHIKKING`, and it follows a procedure of its own, `beroep`, with the stages BEROEP, UITSPRAAK and TOEZENDING.
3. **The Awb hooks onto every appeal.** Awb 6:5 and 6:9 hook onto `BEROEP` as they hook onto `BEZWAAR`: the text of both speaks of the "bezwaar- of beroepschrift".
4. **The names of 6:5 to 6:9 are neutral.** `termijn_einddatum` replaces `bezwaartermijn_einddatum`, and so on for every name the two kinds share. There is no alias for the old names.
5. **The court's judgement is an open norm.** The court decides four things per case, each an open term (`decided_per_case_by`). They are whether the appellant is an interested party, whether the decision breaches a rule of law and which, whether the legal effects stay, and the term for a new decision.
6. **Schema v0.8.10** adds `BEROEP` as a kind of submission and `UITSPRAAK` as a legal character. Nothing else.

### Awb 8:70 and 8:72 in rulework

8:70 lists what the judgment can come to. Its outputs, all on the `UITSPRAAK` item:

| Output | What it is | From |
|---|---|---|
| `beroep_ontvankelijk` | On time (6:9), the contested decision was taken on objection (`taken_on` is 7:11), and the appellant is an interested party | 6:9, 7:1 lid 1, 8:1 |
| `beroep_gegrond` | Null when not admissible; otherwise whether the court finds a rule breached | 8:70 onder c and d, 8:77 lid 2 |
| `uitspraak_strekt_tot` | "niet-ontvankelijkverklaring", "ongegrondverklaring" or "gegrondverklaring van het beroep" | 8:70 onder b to d |
| `geschonden_geoordeelde_rechtsregel` | The rule the judgment names when the appeal is well founded | 8:77 lid 2 |
| `bestreden_besluit_vernietigd` | Annulled, when well founded | 8:72 lid 1 |
| `rechtsgevolgen_vernietigd` | The legal effects go too, unless the court keeps them | 8:72 lid 2 and lid 3 onder a |
| `opdracht_nieuw_besluit` | The administrative body must take a new decision | 8:72 lid 4 |
| `termijn_nieuw_besluit_einddatum` | The last day the court gives for it, if any | 8:72 lid 4 onder b |

The last four come from 8:72, which 8:70 reads as an input from the same law. It reads them only when the appeal is well founded: that is where 8:72 applies ("Indien de bestuursrechter het beroep gegrond verklaart"). The open terms on whether the effects stay and on the term are declared on 8:72, so the court is asked for them only then.

```yaml
# Awb 8:70
produces:
  - type: BEROEP
    requests: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#8:70
    parameters: [bestreden_besluit]
  - type: UITSPRAAK
    outputs: [beroep_ontvankelijk, beroep_gegrond, uitspraak_strekt_tot,
              geschonden_geoordeelde_rechtsregel, bestreden_besluit_vernietigd,
              rechtsgevolgen_vernietigd, opdracht_nieuw_besluit,
              termijn_nieuw_besluit_einddatum]
```

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Appeal received on the last day of the term | On time; the court judges the grounds |
| Appeal received the day after | Not admissible; the court finds nothing |
| Appeal against the decision on an objection, or the new decision it took | Admissible |
| Appeal against a decision on an application, without objection | Not admissible (7:1 lid 1) |
| The appellant is not an interested party | Not admissible (8:1) |
| No rule breached | Unfounded; the decision stands |
| A rule breached, effects not kept, a term set | Well founded, annulled, a new decision within the term |
| A rule breached, effects kept | Well founded, annulled, no new decision |
| The court's judgement on the grounds or on the effects missing | An error, not a silent "unfounded" |

Not modeled:

- competence (8:70 onder a); 8:6 and 8:7 are not in the corpus, so the court is always competent
- the exceptions of 7:1 lid 1 onder b to g, 6:6, 6:9 lid 2 and 6:11
- partial annulment
- the court's own decision in place of the annulled one (8:72 lid 3 onder b)
- 8:72 lid 4 onder a to lid 6
- the court fee (8:41)
- the appeal to the Raad van State (8:104, 8:105)

### Where each choice comes from

| Choice | Source |
|---|---|
| The judgment is not a besluit, so a kind `UITSPRAAK` | The law: Awb 1:1 lid 2 onder c |
| 8:70 establishes the appeal and decides on it | RFC-045, which rejected "6:4 establishes, 7:11 links" for the objection |
| Admissibility: on time, on objection, interested party | Own choice. 8:70 does not say when an appeal is admissible; 6:9, 7:1 lid 1 and 8:1 do, each in part |
| 7:1 as "`taken_on` is 7:11" | Own choice, the mirror of the test in 7:11 (RFC-045) |
| Interested party as an open term on 8:70 | Own choice. 8:1 with 1:2 lid 1 names who may appeal; whether a person is one is the court's judgement |
| Two open terms for the grounds (whether a rule is breached, and which) | Own choice. 8:77 lid 2 asks the judgment to name the breached rule. An open term cannot be a list, and a required one cannot be empty. So whether a rule is breached is a required judgement once the appeal is admissible. The breached rule is an optional text that counts only for a well-founded appeal |
| 8:72 is read by 8:70, not hooked onto the judgment | Own choice. A hook's outputs are not part of the decision value ([RFC-045](/rfcs/rfc-045)), and an annulment that is not an outcome of the judgment cannot reach the school with it |
| Annulment of the whole decision | Own choice: lid 1 also allows a part |
| "Kan" in 8:72 lid 4 as the normal course once lid 3 is not applied | Own choice |
| The court's instructions (lid 4) are not modeled | Owner's decision (20 October 2026) |
| Neutral names for 6:5 to 6:9, without aliases | The law (their text names both), and the owner's decision (20 October 2026). It settles the tradeoff that RFC-050, now part of RFC-045, named for `bezwaartermijn_einddatum` |
| Handing over the copy of the judgment (8:79) is a stage, TOEZENDING | Own choice; the two weeks of 8:79 are not modeled |

## Why

### Benefits

- The appeal needs little code: `BEROEP` joins `SUBMISSION_KINDS` and `UITSPRAAK` the editor's legal characters; 8:70 and 8:72 are rulework.
- The judgment carries the annulment as an outcome, so whatever reads the judgment later reads it from the judgment.
- The names of 6:5 to 6:9 now say what the law says. The end of the term on a decision on objection is the period for appeal, and the name no longer claims otherwise.

### Tradeoffs

- An unstaged execution, such as a scenario, runs at stage BESLUIT, which the procedure `beroep` lacks. Hooks at the stages UITSPRAAK and TOEZENDING fire only in a staged run. None exist yet.
- `geschonden_geoordeelde_rechtsregel` may stay empty on a well-founded appeal. 8:77 lid 2 says the judgment names the rule; nothing enforces that.
- 8:72 applies only when 8:70 reads it. Called on its own, it assumes a well-founded appeal.
- The neutral names change every caller at once: the Awb, its scenarios and those of other laws, the override in the Vreemdelingenwet 2000, the cell's tests, the PoC app and the demo corpus, whose copy of 6:7 and 6:8 must stay equal to the corpus.

### Alternatives Considered

- **8:72 as a hook on the judgment, at stage UITSPRAAK.** The first design had this, after 6:7 on a beschikking. It fails on two counts: the outputs of a hook are not part of the decision value, and an unstaged scenario never reaches the stage.
- **One open term `geschonden_rechtsregel`, null for unfounded.** A required open term cannot be null, and an optional one turns a missing judgement into "unfounded".
- **Keep the old names for the objection and add new ones for the appeal.** Two names for one period of 6:7 would drift.

## Implementation

- Schema v0.8.10: `BEROEP` in `produces.submission.kind`, as a list item's `type` and in `applies_to.submission`; `UITSPRAAK` in `produces.legal_character`, as a list item's `type` and in `applies_to.legal_character`.
- `law-model` and the shared editor code: `BEROEP` in `SUBMISSION_KINDS`. The editor: `UITSPRAAK` in `LEGAL_CHARACTERS`.
- Corpus: the procedure `beroep` in the Awb; 6:5 and 6:9 hook onto `BEROEP`; 7:1 and 8:1 as text; 8:70 and 8:72 executable; 8:77, 8:79, 8:104 and 8:105 as text. `beroep.feature` next to `bezwaar.feature`.
- Not built: the court as a cell of its own, how the school learns of the judgment, the new decision after an order, and the court's own decision in place of the annulled one.
