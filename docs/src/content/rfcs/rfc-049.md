## Context

[RFC-022](/rfcs/rfc-022) §3.2 puts integration configuration on a regulation under `produces.extensions`. On 28 September 2026 the chronolex proof of concept (branch `poc/chronolex`) used this to put the form of a chronolexogram in the law: a block `produces.extensions.chronolex` on each establishing article.

On 7 October 2026 the owner asked why that block was in the law. Chronolexography is how RegelRecht records what follows from a law. A reader of a rulework should not need to know it. Awir 15 says that an application arises. Recording it as a chronolexogram is our choice.

Most of the block follows from keys the law already has. Two things were law content in our own words: the moment that counts, and a special-law parameter that is the specific form of a general-law one.

## Decision

A rulework contains no chronolexography vocabulary: no chronolexogram types, no `produces.extensions.chronolex` block. The law says what arises. The cell derives the record from that. An article that yields an event, such as a payment order, says so with `produces.events`: a name and the output that says when (§5).

### 1. The test for a key

Can you explain the key to a jurist without the words chronolexogram, chronicle (*kroniek*), cell (*cel*) or chronolex? Then it may stand in the rulework. Otherwise it belongs in the stream, the holder's policy or the code. `decides_on` ("this decision is taken on that application") passes. `type: decretogram` fails.

### 2. What the cell derives

| The law says | The cell derives |
|---|---|
| `produces.submission: {kind: AANVRAAG}` ([RFC-046](/rfcs/rfc-046)) | A `submission` chronolexogram, subtype `aanvraag`, stage `AANVRAAG`. Fields: the parameters with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL` ([RFC-048](/rfcs/rfc-048)) of the article and its hooks |
| a hook with `applies_to.submission` | More fields on that chronolexogram |
| `legal_character: BESCHIKKING` with `decides_on` | One decretogram per decision stage (`BESLUIT`, or a stage that `is: BESLUIT`), referring to the submission as `on_application`. Fields: the outputs of the article and its hooks at that stage |
| `legal_character: BESCHIKKING` without `decides_on` | A decretogram that refers to nothing: a decision ex officio, such as the aanslag of AWR 11 |
| `produces.moment` (§3) | The moment that counts (`effective_at`) |
| the establishing article | The chronolexogram's `legal_basis`. Per field, `field_basis` gives the `origin.grondslag` of its parameter or the article that outputs it |
| one date in the `requires` of a decision stage, or `produces.moment.parameter` | The decision date (`dated_by`). The cell fills it with the day of the decision |
| `origin` with `rol: TIJDVAK` | The period the fact concerns |
| `produces.events` (§5) | An executogram per event, when its `when` output is true. Fields: the parameters with origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL`, and all outputs |

The cell refuses a decision on more than one submission, because it could not tell which one the decision answers.

An article that decides at two stages gives two decretograms. Zorgtoeslagwet 2 decides at VOORSCHOT and at TOEKENNING. The stream then names the stage per event (`stage: VOORSCHOT`).

This table is our own derivation. Another cell may derive differently from the same law.

### 3. `produces.moment`: the moment that counts

An application counts from receipt (Awb 4:13 lid 1). A decision counts from the day it is taken.

```yaml
# Awb 4:13, a hook on every application for a beschikking
produces:
  legal_character: TOETS
  decision_type: GEEN_BESLUIT
  moment:
    legal_basis:
      - algemene_wet_bestuursrecht#4:13 lid 1
```

`moment` has a `legal_basis` and an optional `parameter` that holds the moment. Without a parameter, the administrative body sets the moment. For an application, that is when it records the receipt. Reading Awb 4:13 lid 1 this way is an interpretation, and the rulework says so in a comment.

### 4. `specifies`: one datum, two legal bases

Awb 4:2 lid 1 onder a asks for "de naam van de aanvrager". Wet op de politieke partijen 102 lid 3 onder a asks for "de statutaire naam van de politieke partij". The party is the applicant (Awb 1:3 lid 3), so this is one datum with two legal bases.

```yaml
- name: statutaire_naam
  type: string
  origin: {waarde: BELANGHEBBENDE, grondslag: 'wet_op_de_politieke_partijen#102 lid 3'}
  specifies: {regulation: algemene_wet_bestuursrecht, article: '4:2', parameter: naam_aanvrager}
```

A cell records one field for both. This replaces the aliases of the proof of concept. [RFC-003](/rfcs/rfc-003)'s `open_terms` and `implements` do not fit: they fill a value the law leaves open, from a lower layer.

### 5. `produces.events`: what executing an article yields

A payment or a bank's answer is neither an application nor a decision. The law format says it with `produces.events` (schema v0.8.0):

```yaml
produces:
  events:
    - name: betaalopdracht_gegeven
      when: opdracht_wordt_gegeven   # a boolean output of the article
```

Executing the article yields the event when that output is true. One article can yield several events, each on its own output: the bank's terms yield `overboeking_bijgeschreven` or `overboeking_geweigerd`. An event has a name and a `when`, nothing else. It names no type, no fields and no period.

The cell derives the rest. The type is `executogram`. The fields are what the article was given from outside (origin `BELANGHEBBENDE` or `KANAAL`) and all its outputs, because the record holds all the article establishes and a reader selects on the event.

What the cell keeps about recording it is not law and sits in the stream (§6): the chronolexogram it refers to (`refers_to`), the period it concerns (`period`), the days it is executed on (`executed_on`), how a repeated message is recognised (`identified_by`), and the stage after which nothing arises (`until`). The law says nothing about these. Which decision a payment order belongs to is the holder's booking. So is the year it is booked under. Many events concern no period at all, so `period` is optional in the stream and not part of the schema.

### 6. What the stream says

A stream registers an event: its name, the article it `establishes`, and a `stage` where needed (§2). It also holds the holder's way of working: which policy articles an event `reads` ([RFC-047](/rfcs/rfc-047)), the day of a decision (`decided_on`), the period and subject of a decision ex officio, and for an execution the keys of §5 ([RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050)). The cell refuses those execution keys on a decision or an application.

```yaml
- name: betaalopdracht_gegeven
  establishes: fictief_beleid_termijnbedrag_voorschot#1
  refers_to:
    voorschot: {stage: VOORSCHOT, required: true}
  period: {parameter: berekeningsjaar, unit: year}
  executed_on:
    days: fictief_beleid_termijnbedrag_voorschot#5
    parameter: maand
    once_per: month
  until: {stage: TOEKENNING}
```

### 7. Relation to RFC-022

RFC-022 §1.2 stays: each decision stage is its own chronolexogram. §3.2 narrows: `chronolex` is no longer a namespace on articles of law, and schema v0.8.0 has no `produces.extensions` at all. §1.3 changes: an executogram is an event the policy article yields (`produces.events`), with the event and how it is recorded in `cells/<cell>/streams/`, not in `chronicles/`. RFC-022 has a note pointing here.

## Why

### Benefits

- A rulework reads as law, without recording vocabulary.
- A change in the law changes the record. A new hook on the application adds a field to every application chronolexogram.
- Another runtime can record the same law differently.

### Tradeoffs

- The derivation is code in the cell, out of sight of the article. The [Zorgtoeslag as Chronolexography](/components/zorgtoeslag-chronolexography) page lists the rules.
- The stream of an execution carries more than that of a decision: the reference, the period and the days are the holder's, not the law's.
- Schema v0.8.0 accepts `moment` and `specifies` (it leaves `produces` open) but does not describe them.

### Alternatives Considered

**The explicit block on every establishing article** (the proof of concept). It worked, and the creator of Chronolexografie agreed with it. Rejected: it puts our vocabulary in the law, and almost all of it can be derived.

**`effective_at` and aliases as they were.** The content is law, the words were ours. Replaced by `moment` and `specifies`.

**A stage per article** (`stage: BESLUIT` in the block). `is` on a stage ([RFC-008](/rfcs/rfc-008)) already says which stages are decisions. A stage per article cannot express an article that decides twice.

**`legal_character: ANDERE_HANDELING` with `moment` for an execution.** Rejected: it says what kind of act an article is, not that it yields an event, and one article can yield two.

**An explicit block on the policy article for executions only.** The first version of this RFC kept `produces.extensions.chronolex` there, with `type`, `fields`, `refers_to`, `period`, `record_when` and the keys of [RFC-050](/rfcs/rfc-050). Rejected: it kept chronolexography as a dependency of the law format. `produces.events` says the part that is law.

**A type, field list or period on `produces.events`.** Rejected: the type and the fields follow from the article, and a period is the holder's booking that many events do not have.

## Implementation in this pull request

PR #1689, on top of PR #1679, builds the derivation in `packages/cel` (`extension::derive`, `shape`) and `moment` and `specifies` in `packages/law-model`. It adds `produces.events` to schema v0.8.0 and the law-model, and removes `produces.extensions` from both. It removes every `extensions.chronolex` block from `corpus/regulation` and `corpus/demo`, and the cell no longer reads one. No article uses `specifies` yet, and the derivation does not read it.

## Open Questions

1. **Events in statute.** Only fictitious policy uses `produces.events` so far. Does a statutory fact that is neither an application nor a decision (a payment, Awb 4:87; a notice, Awb 3:41) need more than a name and a `when`?
2. **The schema.** A proposal to describe `produces.moment` and `specifies` is still to be written.
3. **A decision on more than one submission**, if a law ever takes one decision on two applications.
