# Claims trace: Rules as Executed, uitgelegd

This file is the claim-by-claim trace from the plain-language explainer page (`/research/rules-as-executed/uitgelegd` and `/explained`) to the position paper "Rules as Executed" as published on 31 August 2026. Each row pairs a claim the page makes (paraphrased from the Dutch source in `paper-explained-content.ts`, under its content key) with the paper section and its anchor in `docs/src/research/rules-as-executed.html`, a short verbatim quote that carries the claim, and a verdict. The paper is frozen; this file and `paper-explained-content.ts` are not, so update the two together: a sentence on the page that has no row here, or whose verdict is not "supported", "page's own example", "page fact" or "vocabulary", needs either a source in the paper or a rewrite.

Verdicts: **supported**; **stronger than paper** (the page says more than the quoted sentence); **hedge dropped** (the paper qualifies, the page does not); **misattributed** (the paper says it of something else); **contradicts paper**; **page's own example** (an illustration the paper does not contain, checked only for being labeled as made up); **page fact** (a statement about the page, its demo or the corpus, not about the paper); **vocabulary** (the page says *regelwerk* where the paper says *executable specification*, a deliberate choice under RFC-044 and not a fidelity question).

**Revision.** This trace was rewritten for the seven-step text (published, power, binding, receipt, default, limits, veterans), which replaced the nine-step text. The trace of that earlier text, with its record of which sentences the first fidelity review had corrected, stays in the git history of this file. A first pass over the seven-step text found sixteen sentences that said more than the paper, misattributed a claim or contradicted it; those were rewritten on the page, and the rows below describe the text after that rewrite. The same revision replaced the made-up parking case of step 1 with the toeslagpartner case, split beleidsregels from werkinstructies in the flow, and added the policymakers and drafters to step 2 and the matrix. Every quoted page sentence was checked against the content file after the last text change, and the three rows the review of f6523c77 named as stale now quote the current wording. Rows whose verdict is not one of the four accepted ones are open: the sentence on the page still needs a rewrite.

**Second revision.** After a reading with a co-author of the paper the page was reordered and extended, and this trace with it. The parts are no longer called steps and carry no number, so the sections below are named after the content key. The problem now opens the page (`problem`, formerly `published`), and the constitutional argument has a part of its own (`power`), which takes the fourth demand out of the first sheet and adds the Grondwet articles, the toeslagenaffaire and the monopoly. `binding` names the binding of publication to execution as the core, and says the format serves it. `time` is new and covers versions and re-execution at a date. The housemate example returns in `power.case` and `binding.case`, and `time` and `limits` have an example each. Rows that only changed key are unchanged in substance.

**Third revision.** A co-author's written comments on the second revision. The page no longer says that rules "decide": the computer executes them and a decision is worked out with them. `power.case` is now the toeslagenaffaire per branch (Section 14) instead of the housemates, whose example stays in `problem` and `binding`. `binding` names what is bound, calls a regelwerk not a model kept alongside the software, and gains `form`: why the binding asks for a format of its own (isomorphism, encoding once, termination, no compiler between published and executed), with the parking example moved out of the sheet to sit under it. `time.intro[2]` brings version control in the manner of Git out of the sheet. `default.variants[1]` names who may depart and the ground. `receipt.limits[0]` says what a recipient can do with the inputs. `problem.demand` now uses the comparison with the law as enacted, `problem.more[1]` describes Robodebt and Horizon, `problem.caseNote` says the two systems are made up, `power.demands[3]` introduces the older demands before adding one, and `veterans.p2` and `veterans.reasons[2]` say why the binding shapes the format and who the readers are. `binding.reuse` (encoding a law once) sits apart from `form`, because the paper does not give it as a reason for the format, and `binding.more[1]` adds the paper's case against linked data. A second fidelity review of this revision found twelve points; the rows below describe the text after those were applied. Points from the comments that the paper does not carry (RegelRecht set against business rules, a position "unique" against ALEF) are not on the page; the paper says its choice "is not a judgment that ALEF was wrong".
